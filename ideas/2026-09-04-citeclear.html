SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-04

CiteClear

AI turns a state health inspector's Form 2567 deficiency citations into a CMS-compliant Plan of Correction — before the 10-day clock triggers a payment freeze.

Target Customer

Administrators and Directors of Nursing at skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) — especially small independent operators and regional chains of 3–15 buildings without an in-house compliance department — who just received a Statement of Deficiencies from their annual (or complaint-triggered) state survey.

Pain Point

Every one of the roughly 15,000 Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing homes in the US gets an unannounced state survey at least once a year, and cannot opt out. The resulting Form CMS-2567 lists deficiencies by F-tag and a severity/scope grid, and the facility has exactly 10 calendar days to submit an acceptable Plan of Correction (PoC) or face escalating consequences: Denial of Payment for New Admissions (a revenue freeze), Civil Monetary Penalties that accrue daily, or in severe cases termination from Medicare/Medicaid entirely. First-draft PoCs are frequently rejected for vague or boilerplate language under CMS's revised, stricter review process, which restarts the clock. Compliance consultants who write acceptable PoCs charge $3,000–$10,000 per cycle and are rarely available on a few days' notice, leaving small operators scrambling every single survey cycle.

MVP Workflow

  1. Administrator uploads the Statement of Deficiencies PDF the moment it's received and enters the survey exit date, starting a 10-day countdown with escalating email/SMS alerts.
  2. Document AI parses each cited deficiency into its F-tag, severity/scope grid level, and the surveyor's specific factual findings and cited resident/record numbers.
  3. Each F-tag is matched against a tagged library of CMS-accepted PoC language (built from State Operations Manual guidance and historically accepted, de-identified PoC submissions) and against the specific facts cited, to draft a tailored response covering all four CMS-required elements: corrective action for affected residents, corrective action for others potentially affected, systemic/monitoring changes, and a completion date.
  4. Deficiencies likely to carry Immediate Jeopardy exposure or high CMP risk are flagged separately so the facility loops in legal counsel before submitting rather than after.
  5. System outputs a formatted PoC ready for the state agency's submission channel (portal or fax), as an editable draft the administrator reviews, adjusts, and signs.
  6. Each facility's F-tag history is logged over time so repeat citations — which draw materially harsher penalties on a follow-up survey — are surfaced before the next survey window opens.

Data & Integrations Needed

CMS Form 2567 parsing (Document AI/OCR) State Operations Manual & F-tag regulatory text corpus Library of historically accepted PoC language CMS Care Compare deficiency database (public) Deadline engine with email/SMS alerts State survey-agency e-fax/portal submission

Pricing Model

Per-survey-cycle fee of $399–$899, scaled by the number and severity of deficiencies cited, with 48-hour turnaround — matching the reality that surveys land unpredictably roughly once a year rather than fitting a monthly subscription. An optional $99/mo "compliance monitor" add-on tracks the facility's F-tag trends against Care Compare data for peer facilities in-state and proactively flags recurring risk areas before the next survey.

First Version Scope

Cover federally certified skilled nursing facilities only (skip assisted living, which is state-licensed with too much cross-state variation for v1), and standard severity/scope deficiencies (Level D–F) rather than Immediate Jeopardy citations, which need a legal-review-gated workflow. Ship deficiency parsing plus drafted PoC first; defer repeat-citation trend tracking to v1.1. Go to market through state Health Care Association affiliates, LeadingAge chapters, and SNF administrator Facebook/LinkedIn groups, plus direct outreach timed against publicly posted CMS survey data showing which facilities were just surveyed.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

CMS publishes the complete, structured national deficiency and F-tag history for every certified nursing home via Care Compare — exactly the kind of labeled, citable corpus most compliance categories lack, and one competitors mine for sales leads but not for drafting the response itself. The market is also self-selecting by federal design: 100% of SNFs are surveyed annually, cannot opt out, and face a hard 10-day federal deadline the instant paper lands, which is a far stronger and more predictable purchase trigger than most compliance software gets. And because most small operators treat this as an annual fire drill rather than an ongoing tool, per-incident pricing that mirrors how the pain actually arrives outperforms the subscription-first model that generic long-term-care compliance SaaS defaults to.
