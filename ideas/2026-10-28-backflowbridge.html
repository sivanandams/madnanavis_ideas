BackflowBridge

Turns a certified backflow tester's field readings into the exact report format, portal submission, and due-date reminder each water utility demands — so one tester stops re-typing the same test into 40 different forms.

Target Customer

Solo and small-crew certified backflow assembly testers (and small irrigation/fire-sprinkler/plumbing shops that employ one tester), each servicing 300–3,000 devices across several water districts and municipalities. Secondary buyer: the customers' property managers who just want to know their devices are compliant.

Pain Point

  • Every utility requires an annual test report on a device it owns the compliance record for, but each has its own PDF form, emailed address, fax, or clunky portal — and a test with a failed device needs a follow-up retest report within a strict window.
  • Testers write numbers on a carbon-copy tag on-site, then spend evenings re-keying the same readings (check valve psi, relief valve opening, serial, location) into whichever format the utility wants. Missed or malformed submissions mean the property owner gets a shutoff notice and blames the tester.
  • Utility-run compliance software serves the water district, not the tester; generic field-service apps have no concept of "this district rejects reports without the device's 5-digit utility tag number."

MVP Workflow

  1. Tester photographs the device and gauge-kit readout, or types/dictates the readings in the phone app (offline-capable; works in basements and mechanical rooms).
  2. Vision + rules extract serial, size, model, location, and pass/fail logic (e.g., check valve ≥ 1.0 psid, relief opening ≥ 2.0 psid) and flag readings that look like transcription errors.
  3. The tester picks the device's utility (remembered per address); the app fills that utility's form template, or pushes it through the utility's portal/email intake, and stores the submitted PDF + confirmation.
  4. A failed device auto-creates a repair/retest task with the utility's deadline and a customer-facing notice.
  5. Annual-due dates roll forward per device; the tester gets a route-ordered "due in 60 days" list and one-tap customer reminders.

Data & Integrations Needed

  • Per-utility form templates and rejection rules (seeded by hand for the first 10 local utilities; crowd-corrected by users afterward).
  • Phone camera/OCR, device-reading calculator, offline sync.
  • Email-out and headless-browser submission to utility portals where no API exists; CSV/XML import where a utility offers one.
  • Optional: QuickBooks/Stripe for invoicing, Google Maps for route ordering, SMS for customer reminders.

Pricing Model

Solo $49/mo up to 500 devices   Crew $129/mo up to 3 testers, 3,000 devices   Per-utility add-on $15/mo for portal auto-submit on a non-standard utility. 30-day trial with free import of the tester's existing spreadsheet.

First Version Scope

  • iOS/Android PWA with offline test entry and photo capture for reduced-pressure, double-check, and pressure-vacuum-breaker assemblies.
  • Support for 5–10 utilities in a single metro area, with form-fill to PDF + email submission (no portal automation yet).
  • Device registry with annual-due tracking and failed-test retest deadlines.
  • Spreadsheet import; no invoicing, no customer portal, no inventory in v1.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The product isn't a field-service app — it's a translation layer between one tester's measurements and dozens of idiosyncratic regulators' intake formats. The moat is the curated, user-corrected library of utility-specific submission rules (what each district rejects and why), which compounds with every tester added to a region and is invisible to horizontal FSM vendors. AI does the narrow jobs it's good at: reading gauge photos, catching impossible readings, and normalizing messy utility forms — not chatting.
