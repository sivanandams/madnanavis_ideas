SaaS Idea Log — 2026-10-18

HoodProof

Photo-first compliance records for commercial kitchen exhaust-hood cleaners: AI grades before/after grease photos against NFPA 96 and auto-sends fire-marshal- and insurer-ready proof to every restaurant client.

Target Customer

Independent kitchen exhaust cleaning (KEC) contractors — 1 to 15 technicians servicing restaurants, hospitals, school cafeterias and hotels on quarterly, semi-annual or annual NFPA 96 cycles. Roughly 5,000+ small US shops, most running on paper tags, phone photos and spreadsheets.

Pain Point

After a grease fire, the insurer and fire marshal ask the restaurant for proof that the hood, plenum, duct and fan were actually cleaned to bare metal. Cleaners typically hand over a sticker and an invoice; the proof is a messy camera roll. Disputes over "you skipped the roof fan" cost shops their accounts, and insurers increasingly deny claims when records are thin. Meanwhile, owners lose recurring revenue because they track due dates by memory, and techs cut corners on inaccessible duct sections nobody can verify. Generic field-service tools (Jobber, Housecall Pro) have no concept of NFPA 96 sections, access panels, or grease-depth evidence.

MVP Workflow

  1. Owner creates a site with its hood/duct/fan "zones" from a template (hood, filters, plenum, horizontal duct, vertical riser, rooftop fan) and its required cleaning frequency, driven by cooking type (e.g. solid fuel monthly, high-volume quarterly, moderate semi-annual).
  2. The tech's phone app walks a checklist per zone, requiring a before and after photo with GPS and timestamp; it prompts for missing access-panel shots.
  3. A vision model scores each after-photo for residual grease (pass / re-clean / unclear) and flags before-photos showing heavy buildup that warrants a recommended-frequency change or a "not cleanable without new access panel" deficiency note.
  4. On completion, the system generates a branded, tamper-evident PDF report plus a QR code on the service sticker that links to the client's public record, and emails it to the restaurant, their insurer contact, and optionally the local fire marshal.
  5. A scheduler reminds the client and auto-proposes the next visit date; deficiency items become upsell quotes (access panels, fan hinge kits).

Data & Integrations Needed

NFPA 96 frequency & zone rules (static config) Phone camera + GPS + timestamp Vision model for grease grading QuickBooks / Stripe invoicing Email / SMS delivery Hashed record storage for tamper evidence

Pricing Model

$79/mo per shop plus $19/mo per technician seat, with a $2 per-site-visit record-hosting fee that scales with usage. An optional $49/mo "insurer portal" tier gives restaurant clients and their insurance brokers a permanent lookup page, which cleaners can resell as a premium service line.

First Version Scope

iOS/Android-friendly web app (PWA) with offline photo capture, one fixed NFPA 96 checklist template, manual pass/fail by the tech with the AI grade as an advisory second opinion, PDF report plus QR record page, and email reminders. Skip scheduling optimization, invoicing, and fire-marshal integrations for v1. Go to market through IKECA-certified cleaner communities, Facebook groups for hood cleaners, and supplier trade shows; offer a done-for-you import of the first 20 client sites.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The buyer isn't the restaurant, it's the cleaner — but the real value is created for a third party (the insurer) who never pays. Making the cleaner's evidence trail the product turns a cost-of-doing-business chore into a differentiator when pitching clients, and the QR sticker turns every cleaned hood into a free advertisement. The AI is deliberately narrow: not a chatbot, but a grease-depth grader on a single, repetitive image type, which is feasible with a small labeled dataset the product itself accumulates — a data moat generic field-service apps can't build.
