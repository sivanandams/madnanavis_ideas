SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-14

MilestoneGuard

AI tracks state-mandated milestone structural inspection and reserve-study deadlines for condo/HOA boards, ingests the engineer's report, and auto-drafts the legally required owner disclosure packet before the fine clock — or the insurance non-renewal — hits.

Target Customer

Volunteer condo and HOA board treasurers/presidents, and the community association managers (CAMs) who serve small-to-mid buildings (roughly 3–25 stories, self-managed or under a small CAM firm rather than a national management giant), concentrated in states with new post-Surfside structural inspection statutes — Florida (SB 4-D / §553.899), California (SB 326 balcony inspections, SB 721), and the wave of similar bills moving through other coastal and high-rise states.

Pain Point

Since the 2021 Surfside collapse, states have imposed strict, statute-specific deadlines: milestone structural inspections at fixed building ages (e.g. 30 years, or 25 years within 3 miles of the coast, then every 10 years in Florida), adequately funded reserve studies, and mandatory owner disclosure within a set number of days of receiving the engineer's report — all backed by civil liability, county fines, and insurers now flatly refusing to renew or quote coverage without proof of compliance. Volunteer board members with full-time day jobs are expected to track which statute applies to their exact building, translate a 40-page engineering report full of technical severity language into a legally compliant disclosure letter, mail it correctly, and keep proof — and in practice most boards only discover they've blown a deadline when a renewal notice comes back as a non-renewal or a unit sale falls through at closing because the buyer's attorney can't get a compliance packet.

MVP Workflow

  1. Board or CAM enters the building's address, year built, story count, and distance from the coast; the engine matches it against a curated statute database and computes the exact applicable inspection and reserve-study deadlines, county by county.
  2. A deadline dashboard shows every upcoming trigger and fires email/SMS alerts at 12, 6, 3, and 1 month out, plus a separate alert tied to the association's insurance renewal date.
  3. Board uploads the engineer's milestone inspection report (PDF); document AI extracts the findings, severity classification (e.g. "substantial structural deterioration"), and any required remediation timeline.
  4. The system auto-generates the state-specific owner disclosure notice — correctly formatted, citing the right statute section, summarizing findings within the mandated timeframe — ready for board signature and mailing.
  5. It compiles a compliance binder (PDF): engineer report, disclosure letters, reserve study summary, and a proof-of-mailing log — the exact packet insurers, mortgage lenders, and closing attorneys ask for.

Data & Integrations Needed

Curated state statute & county deadline database County assessor / GIS building-age records Document AI for engineering-report ingestion Certified-mail / e-delivery API (e.g. Lob) for owner notices CSV import/export for AMS platforms (Vantaca, AppFolio, Buildium) Deadline engine with email/SMS alerts

Pricing Model

Per-building annual subscription, $600–$1,800/yr scaled by unit count, billed to the association (paid by the board treasurer directly or passed through a CAM firm's invoicing). Add a one-time $299 "compliance rescue" fee for boards that discover they're already past a deadline and need an expedited disclosure packet.

First Version Scope

Launch with Florida only (SB 4-D / §553.899 is the most litigated and urgent statute), targeting self-managed and small-CAM buildings. Ship deadline tracking and disclosure-letter generation from a manually uploaded engineer PDF; skip automated reserve-study math (refer out to licensed reserve-study firms for v1) and skip OCR of on-site inspection photos. Go to market through Florida CAI/CAM chapters, condo-law attorney referral partnerships, and property-and-casualty insurance agents who need their book of business insurable.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The obvious read is "boring compliance software a big PropTech player eventually eats" — but the real wedge isn't the reserve-study math, which is a licensed-engineer judgment call no AI should touch. It's the unglamorous, entirely deterministic choke point of turning a dense engineering PDF into the exact statute-mandated notice letter, on time, in the right format — precisely the kind of rules-bound document generation AI is well suited for and where unpaid volunteer boards reliably fail. And because non-compliance now surfaces as an insurance non-renewal or a stalled closing rather than a board's own to-do list, the highest-intent buyer often isn't the board at all — it's the insurance agent or the closing attorney who needs a compliance packet to keep a policy bound or a sale moving, giving the product a second, non-obvious referral channel with its own urgency independent of the board.
