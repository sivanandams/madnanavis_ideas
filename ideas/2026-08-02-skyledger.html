SkyLedger

Auto-builds the FAA compliance paper trail for solo and small commercial drone operators — airspace authorization, pilot currency, and per-flight documentation — so a ramp check, insurance renewal, or client audit never catches them empty-handed.

Target Customer

Solo and small-team commercial drone pilots operating under FAA Part 107 — real estate/aerial photographers, roof and property inspectors, construction-progress and agriculture/crop-scouting operators, and 2–5-pilot drone service shops that fly regularly in or near controlled airspace.

Pain Point

Part 107 operators must be able to produce, on demand, a coherent record of LAANC/controlled-airspace authorizations, remote pilot certificate currency (recurrent training every 24 months), aircraft registration, and per-flight conditions — but almost none of this is centralized. Most solo operators track it, if at all, in scattered app screenshots, a spreadsheet, or nothing. The gap only surfaces at the worst moment: an FAA ramp check or incident inquiry, a commercial client (real estate brokerage, engineering firm, insurer) demanding proof of compliance before hiring, or a liability-insurance renewal that now requires a documented compliance history. At that point missing records mean a denied claim, a lost contract, or a certificate action — not a $50 fine — which makes this a "keep me legal" purchase rather than a nice-to-have.

MVP Workflow

  1. Pilot connects their drone's flight-log export (DJI is the dominant OEM format) or logs a flight manually from the mobile web app after landing.
  2. System cross-references each flight's time and GPS track against LAANC/UAS Facility Map airspace data to confirm the flight was authorized, and flags any flight over controlled airspace, over people, or beyond visual line of sight that lacks a matching authorization or waiver on file.
  3. System checks the pilot's FAA airman registry status and recurrent-training date and surfaces a running currency badge (green/amber/red).
  4. Each flight is auto-packaged into a "flight record" — airspace authorization proof, weather conditions at launch time pulled from a public weather API, aircraft registration, and pilot certificate status at time of flight.
  5. 60/30/7-day reminders fire before recurrent training lapses or aircraft registration expires.
  6. On demand — insurance renewal, new client onboarding, FAA inquiry — the pilot generates a single "compliance packet" PDF covering a date range or specific job.

Data & Integrations Needed

DJI flight-log / generic CSV telemetry parser FAA LAANC & UAS Facility Map airspace data FAA airman registry lookup NOAA/weather API for point-in-time conditions PDF packet generation Email/SMS reminders

Pricing Model

$39/mo per solo pilot, $129/mo for small shops (up to 5 pilots) with shared aircraft and job records, plus a $15 one-off compliance-packet export for occasional flyers who don't want a subscription. Priced as a rounding error next to a liability-insurance premium or a lost client contract.

First Version Scope

DJI log import plus manual flight entry only; LAANC cross-checking limited to the 20 busiest US metro airspace zones; recurrent-training and registration reminders; single compliance-packet PDF export. No BVLOS/state-specific waiver tracking and no direct insurer integrations in v1 — those come once packet-export usage validates demand. Distribute through drone-service Facebook groups, r/drones commercial threads, and partnerships with the liability insurers (e.g., BWI, SkyWatch) who already require this documentation at renewal.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Nearly all drone software investment targets the flying layer — mapping, photogrammetry, fleet ops (DroneDeploy, Skyward) — built for enterprise fleets, not the long tail of one-truck operators. SkyLedger targets the opposite layer: proving you were legal, not flying better. The buying trigger isn't pilot ambition, it's an external forcing function — an insurer, a commercial client, or the FAA itself demanding documentation that didn't exist five minutes earlier — which makes this a check-the-box purchase with low price sensitivity and high renewal stickiness, in a segment (solo/small operators) too fragmented and low-ARPU for the enterprise drone-ops vendors to bother chasing.
