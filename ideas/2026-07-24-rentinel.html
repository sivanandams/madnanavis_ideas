SaaS Idea Log — 2026-07-24

Rentinel

AI reads commercial leases, then reconciles CAM billing and escalation math against what tenants are actually being charged — catching missed renewal deadlines and misbilled rent before they cost a small landlord real money.

Target Customer

Independent commercial landlords and small property management firms running 5–50 tenants across strip malls, office parks, and mixed-use retail — portfolios too small to justify MRI, Yardi, or Argus lease-administration modules, so lease abstracts and CAM math live in scanned PDFs, memory, and a bookkeeper's spreadsheet.

Pain Point

Every commercial lease buries critical terms — renewal option windows, rent escalation triggers, CAM/expense-stop caps, base-year resets, co-tenancy clauses — inside dense legal language, often across multiple amendments. At this portfolio size there's no lease-admin system tracking any of it, so renewal options silently lapse (losing an above-market tenant or worse, locking the landlord into a below-market rate for years), escalations get invoiced at the wrong amount, and CAM reconciliations skip caps or use a stale base year. These errors are invisible until a tenant disputes a bill or an attorney flags a missed deadline during a sale — by which point it's a five- or six-figure problem instead of a five-minute fix.

MVP Workflow

  1. Landlord or property manager uploads lease PDFs — new leases, renewals, and amendments — to Rentinel.
  2. Document AI extracts a structured abstract: parties, term, renewal option windows and notice deadlines, base rent and escalation schedule, CAM/expense-stop terms (cap %, base year, exclusions), and co-tenancy/exclusivity clauses.
  3. Landlord imports their rent roll or accounting export (QuickBooks, AppFolio CSV, or plain spreadsheet) showing what tenants were actually billed.
  4. A reconciliation engine cross-references the lease abstract against actual billing and flags mismatches — an escalation that wasn't applied on schedule, a CAM charge that exceeded the contractual cap, a base year used incorrectly.
  5. A deadline calendar auto-generates 90/60/30-day alerts ahead of every renewal option window and required tenant notice.
  6. For each flagged discrepancy, Rentinel drafts a correction memo or tenant billing-adjustment letter citing the exact clause and dollar impact.

Data & Integrations Needed

Document AI for lease/amendment parsing CSV / QuickBooks / AppFolio rent-roll import CAM & escalation rules engine Deadline calendar with email/SMS alerts Letter/memo generation Clause library by lease type (NNN, gross, modified gross)

Pricing Model

Per-active-lease subscription, roughly $15–25/month per lease abstracted and monitored, tiered by portfolio size — the price scales directly with the number of leases under management, and a single caught CAM error or saved renewal option pays for a year of the subscription outright.

First Version Scope

Support standard retail and office NNN/gross leases with simple percentage or CPI escalations (defer ground leases and exotic escalation formulas to v2). Manual PDF upload plus CSV/rent-roll import only — no live PM-software sync. Ship renewal-deadline alerting and CAM/escalation reconciliation as the two flagship features; defer co-tenancy and exclusivity clause monitoring to a later release. Go to market directly through commercial real estate broker networks and small PM firm associations.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Enterprise lease-administration suites already solve this problem — for portfolios of hundreds of properties, at six-figure implementation cost. The 5–50 property landlord, which is the numerical majority of U.S. commercial real estate ownership, is considered too small a deal for that market and is left with Excel and institutional memory. And unlike a generic "AI reads your contract" tool, the wedge isn't summarization — it's the reconciliation step that cross-references extracted lease terms against actual billed amounts to surface a specific, dollar-denominated error. That turns the pitch from "helpful document reader" into "we found you $14,000 you're owed," which sells itself on the very first upload instead of requiring an education campaign.
