SaaS Idea Log — 2026-07-31

WattClaim

AI pulls live production data straight from your solar inverter's API and checks it against your installer's contracted performance guarantee — then auto-files the shortfall claim before the deadline quietly passes.

Target Customer

Homeowners with residential solar systems installed under a dealer or financier contract that includes a production/performance guarantee — common with SunPower, Sunnova, Sunrun, Vivint Solar, and regional dealer-financed installs. Roughly a third of the 7M+ US residential solar systems carry some form of guaranteed-output clause tied to financing.

Pain Point

Performance-guarantee clauses obligate the installer (or its financier) to pay cash for any shortfall between guaranteed and actual annual kWh production, checked on the contract's anniversary date. Almost no homeowner tracks this: they don't know the guaranteed number, don't compute actual output against it, and miss the narrow filing window each year. Meanwhile a wave of installer bankruptcies (Pink Energy, SunPower, Sunworks, and others since 2022) has scattered the actual obligor across successor financiers and loan servicers, so even homeowners who suspect they're owed money don't know who to bill. The data to prove the claim already lives in the monitoring app on their phone — nobody is turning it into a dollar figure and a letter.

MVP Workflow

  1. Homeowner connects their monitoring account (Enphase Enlighten, SolarEdge, Tesla, etc.) via OAuth — no hardware access needed, just the existing consumer app login.
  2. Homeowner uploads their installation contract or PPA; document AI extracts the guaranteed annual kWh figure, the true-up/anniversary date, and the shortfall payment formula.
  3. System pulls daily production data from the monitoring API for the contract year and computes actual vs. guaranteed output.
  4. If a shortfall exists, it calculates the dollar amount owed per the contract's own formula and generates a claim letter citing the exact clause, the production data, and the computed amount.
  5. If the original installer is defunct, the report cross-references a maintained bankruptcy/successor database to identify the correct financier, servicer, or state solar-recovery fund to file against instead.
  6. The system re-runs automatically each year on the guarantee anniversary and nudges the homeowner with a pre-filled claim rather than waiting to be asked.

Data & Integrations Needed

Enphase Enlighten API SolarEdge Monitoring API Tesla Energy API Document AI for contract parsing Installer bankruptcy / successor-servicer database Annual scheduling & reminder engine Letter/PDF generation

Pricing Model

Contingency-first: no charge unless a shortfall claim is found, then 20% of the recovered amount, capped at $150 per claim — removes signup friction and lets the pitch be "we found you're owed $X" rather than "pay us to check." An optional $49/year flat plan is offered for owners who'd rather not share claim payouts once trust is established.

First Version Scope

Support Enphase and SolarEdge only (roughly two-thirds of residential US installs), and only contracts with an explicit, extractable production-guarantee clause — skip plain cash-purchase systems with no guarantee. One claim-letter template, manual contract upload, single-year true-up calculation (no multi-year averaging clauses in v1). Distribute through r/solar, solar-owner Facebook groups, and referral partnerships with consumer attorneys who already field these complaints but don't have the data-pulling tooling to quantify them.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Nearly all solar software targets the installer side — design tools, lead gen, O&M dashboards — because that's where the money and distribution have historically been. WattClaim inverts the relationship: it treats the installer's own guarantee clause as a collections target on behalf of the homeowner, which is exactly the adversarial use case no installer-funded tool will ever build. It's also structurally different from document-only reconciliation ideas — the core signal is live production telemetry pulled via API, cross-referenced deterministically against a contract term, which is a natural fit for automation and needs no chatbot UX at all. The installer-bankruptcy wave makes this timelier than it looks: guarantee obligations don't disappear when a company folds, they just become harder to trace, which is precisely the lookup problem a maintained successor database solves.
