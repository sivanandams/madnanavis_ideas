608Trace

AI logs every pound of refrigerant an HVAC/R contractor buys, recovers, and recharges, auto-builds the EPA Section 608 audit trail a shop needs to survive an inspection, and flags leak-rate violations before the EPA does.

Target Customer

Small-to-midsize HVAC/refrigeration contractors (5–50 technicians) who service commercial refrigeration and large comfort-cooling systems above the EPA's 50-lb charge threshold — supermarket and convenience-store refrigeration servicers, cold-storage and food-service equipment maintenance companies, and shops still carrying legacy R-22 systems through the ongoing HFC phase-down.

Pain Point

EPA's Section 608 rules require a contractor to log refrigerant type and quantity added/recovered plus technician certification on every applicable service ticket, track leak rate per appliance, and initiate mandatory repairs once an appliance crosses its leak-rate threshold — with civil penalties running well into five figures per day per violation after EPA's periodic inflation adjustments. Most small shops track this on paper tags, a shared spreadsheet, or nothing at all; techs don't reliably fill in the required fields; and refrigerant is now expensive enough, with the AIM Act's HFC phase-down squeezing supply through 2030, that owners have no real visibility into shrinkage or theft versus legitimate use. When EPA or a state agency shows up — often triggered by a competitor complaint or a former employee — reconstructing two years of compliant records from paper tags is a nightmare, and missing-recordkeeping fines alone can be existential for a small shop.

MVP Workflow

  1. Tech logs each job from a mobile-first form at the point of service: appliance ID/location, refrigerant type, cylinder weigh-in/weigh-out (photo of the scale, OCR'd), work performed, and their 608 cert number.
  2. System cross-checks the entry against that appliance's service history to auto-calculate its rolling annual leak rate and flags any unit crossing the EPA's actionable threshold, auto-generating the required repair timeline and verification-test reminder.
  3. Distributor invoices are forwarded by email and parsed to reconcile cylinder-level refrigerant purchases against recovered/recharged quantities per technician, surfacing variance reports that catch mis-logging or shrinkage before it becomes a compliance blind spot.
  4. One-click "audit binder" export builds the EPA-required recordkeeping packet — technician certs, per-appliance leak logs, disposal/reclamation certificates, purchase records — formatted for inspection, alongside a live dashboard of which appliances and techs are out of compliance right now.
  5. Automated alerts track company-level EPA registration and state license renewals, plus refrigerant-type-specific rule changes as the R-22-to-HFC-to-next-gen transition keeps shifting.

Data & Integrations Needed

Mobile-first offline-capable capture (PWA) OCR for scale readouts & cylinder labels Distributor invoice email-forward parsing EPA Section 608 leak-rate rules engine Reclaimer disposal-certificate tracking Optional FSM/dispatch sync (ServiceTitan, FieldEdge)

Pricing Model

Per-technician SaaS at roughly $39–59/tech/month, priced as audit defense rather than per ticket, plus a company-level base fee (~$99/mo) covering the audit-binder generator and cylinder reconciliation dashboard. A flat "audit rescue" tier ($1,500–3,000) for shops that just got an inspection notice and need two years of paper-tag history digitized and reconciled fast.

First Version Scope

Cover only commercial refrigeration and comfort-cooling appliances above the 50-lb charge threshold — the actual EPA reporting/leak-rate trigger — and skip residential split systems and motor-vehicle AC, which fall under separate, lighter rules. V1 is manual mobile entry plus distributor invoice forwarding only, no FSM integration yet. Ship leak-rate flagging and the audit-binder export first, since those are the two moments a shop owner actually feels the pain: a state inspector's letter, or an insurance/liability review. Go to market through HVAC/R trade associations (RSES, ACCA commercial-refrigeration chapters), 608-cert renewal and training providers, and referral partnerships with refrigerant distributors.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Every dispatch and field-service tool (ServiceTitan, FieldEdge) treats refrigerant as a billable line item, not a regulated substance with its own audit trail — so the compliance layer that actually protects a shop owner from a five- or six-figure EPA fine doesn't exist as a dedicated product, even though the underlying data (cylinder weights, technician certs, per-appliance leak history) is exactly the kind of structured, repetitive capture that AI/OCR handles well and generic chatbot Q&A never touches. The AIM Act's HFC phase-down is actively driving refrigerant scarcity and price spikes through 2030, which means both the compliance risk and the shrinkage/theft risk this product also solves get worse every year rather than fading — a rare case where the regulatory tailwind and the cost-savings pitch point the same direction, so the sale doesn't depend on a shop owner caring about compliance in the abstract.
