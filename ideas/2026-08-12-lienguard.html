LienGuard

AI turns each state's self-storage lien-sale statute into a hard compliance calendar, auto-drafts the certified-mail notices and publication listings, and refuses to unlock the auction until every statutory step is documented — so independent storage operators stop getting sued for botched lien sales.

Target Customer

Independently owned self-storage facilities (non-REIT, typically 1–10 locations) and small vehicle towing/impound lot operators who process lien sales themselves, without in-house counsel or a compliance department.

Pain Point

Every state — and sometimes county or city — sets its own statutory notice period, certified-mail requirements, publication rules, and minimum waiting period before a delinquent tenant's unit can legally be declared abandoned and auctioned. Get one step wrong (wrong notice window, wrong mail method, skipped newspaper publication where required) and the operator faces a wrongful-lien-sale or conversion lawsuit, with real judgments running from low five figures into six figures plus attorney fees. The dominant self-storage management platforms (SiteLink, storEDGE) explicitly disclaim responsibility for lien-sale legal compliance in their terms — they handle rent billing, not the statute — leaving small operators to track 50-state rules by memory or a printed cheat sheet.

MVP Workflow

  1. Operator enters the facility's state/county and the delinquent tenant's default date, plus unit or vehicle details.
  2. System looks up the applicable statute (state self-storage lien act or vehicle abandonment statute) and generates the exact compliance calendar: notice-by date, certified-mail send-by date, publication window, and earliest legal auction date.
  3. Auto-drafts the legally formatted notice letter as a certified-mail-ready document, and where required, the newspaper or online publication listing text.
  4. Operator logs the certified mail tracking number and any publication proof; the system flags anything overdue or under-documented and keeps the auction date locked until every required step is checked off.
  5. On the cleared date, the system generates an auction listing with photos for push to a marketplace, plus a disposition-of-proceeds ledger showing what (if anything) is owed back to the tenant after the sale.

Data & Integrations Needed

50-state self-storage lien act & vehicle abandonment statute database, kept current Certified mail API (e.g. Lob, USPS) Auction marketplace API (e.g. StorageTreasures) Webhook/CSV import from SiteLink or storEDGE for delinquency data PDF generation & e-signature

Pricing Model

Per-lien-case fee of $15–$30 processed, matching how operators actually think about the cost of "that one problem tenant," plus an optional $49/mo base subscription for the compliance dashboard and calendar. No long-term contract — usage tracks delinquency rates, which operators already monitor closely.

First Version Scope

Cover self-storage lien sales only, limited to the ten states with the most facilities (TX, CA, FL, GA, NC, and similar), and defer vehicle towing/impound liens to a later version since they run on a different statute set. Ship with one auction-marketplace integration and manual certified-mail tracking-number entry — skip the mail API until the notice-drafting workflow is validated against real cases. Go to market through self-storage owner-operator Facebook groups, the Self Storage Association's independent-owner segment, and referrals from storage-industry auction and appraisal consultants.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The incumbent storage-management platforms have every incentive to stay away from this exact feature: taking on lien-sale compliance means taking on legal liability they don't want, so their terms of service explicitly disclaim it rather than build it — which means the market leaders structurally cannot compete here even if they wanted to. It's also a case where a dense, unglamorous statute is the entire product: operators don't want to ask an AI questions about lien law, they want software that reads the statute once and then simply refuses to let them auction a unit out of sequence — a hard workflow gate, not a chatbot.
