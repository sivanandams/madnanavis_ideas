GrossGuard

AI parses your franchise agreement's "Gross Sales" definition and reconciles it against your POS exports to catch royalty and ad-fund overpayments — before they compound across every unit, every month.

Target Customer

Multi-unit franchisees (roughly 2–15 units) in food service, fitness, and quick-service retail — sandwich shops, gyms, coffee chains, salons — who pay an ongoing royalty (typically 5–8%) plus a marketing/ad fund fee (2–4%) calculated off "Gross Sales" as defined in their franchise agreement.

Pain Point

Franchise agreements define "Gross Sales" narrowly — usually excluding sales tax, discounts, gift-card issuance (vs. redemption), delivery-platform commissions, and employee meals — but the franchisor's royalty statements and auto-debit systems are often computed off raw POS gross revenue that never applies those exclusions. Multi-unit operators rarely have the legal or accounting bandwidth to cross-check every monthly statement against contract language across every location, and the franchisor has no incentive to self-correct. Overpayments of 0.3–1.5% of gross revenue compound across units and years into tens of thousands of dollars, and by the time an operator commissions forensic accounting — usually only at a sale or renewal — months of recoverable overpayment have often passed the statute-of-limitations window.

MVP Workflow

  1. Franchisee uploads their franchise agreement (or just the royalty/"Gross Sales" clauses); document AI extracts the exact definition — what's included and excluded.
  2. Franchisee exports their POS system's monthly sales summary by unit (Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed).
  3. The system recomputes contract-defined Gross Sales from the granular POS line items, applying the specific inclusion/exclusion rules extracted from the agreement.
  4. A diff engine compares contract-defined Gross Sales × royalty rate against what the franchisor's monthly royalty invoice actually charged, flagging per-unit, per-month variance.
  5. For each flagged discrepancy, the system drafts a recomputation memo citing the exact agreement clause and the specific POS line items that were wrongly included, ready to send to the franchisor's accounting department or use in renewal negotiations.

Data & Integrations Needed

Document AI for franchise agreement clause extraction POS exports/APIs: Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed Rules engine for Gross Sales recomputation Royalty statement (PDF/email) parsing PDF/report generation

Pricing Model

Per-unit monthly subscription (~$39–59/unit/month), since the pain and the value both scale with unit count and this is a recurring check, not a one-time audit. A one-time "historical lookback audit" (~$500–1,500 flat, covering the trailing 12–24 months) works as the higher-value entry point — it pays for itself immediately if it recovers even a few thousand dollars, and converts naturally into the subscription.

First Version Scope

Support the 1–2 most common POS systems for the initial target vertical (Toast for food-service franchisees first), with manual agreement upload and founder-reviewed AI clause extraction while building a reusable clause library across common franchise brands. Ship the monthly variance report before automating direct royalty-statement ingestion. Recruit 2–3 franchise systems known for complex Gross Sales definitions as design partners, and distribute through multi-unit franchisee associations, franchise broker/consultant networks, and franchisee-only Facebook and forum groups.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Existing franchise compliance software (FranConnect, Naranga) is built for franchisors to monitor franchisees — nobody builds the inverse: tooling that helps franchisees audit their franchisor. The information asymmetry is structural, since the franchisor writes the contract and controls the royalty math, and franchisees have historically had no scalable way to check the arithmetic themselves, only expensive one-off forensic accounting during a sale or dispute. Because "Gross Sales" exclusions are deterministic, rules-based math against structured POS data, this is a precise reconciliation problem rather than a conversational one — and multi-unit operators already think in per-unit monthly costs, making a per-unit subscription a natural sell rather than a hard one.
