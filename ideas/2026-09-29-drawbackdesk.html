SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-29

DrawbackDesk

Match a small importer's tariff-paid entries to their export shipments and auto-assemble the U.S. customs duty drawback claim that refunds up to 99% of duties on goods later re-exported.

Target Customer

Small and mid-size U.S. importer-exporters ($2M–$100M in annual imports) who bring in components, materials or finished goods and ship some of them abroad — contract manufacturers, equipment makers, distributors selling into Canada/Mexico/LatAm, and DTC brands with international fulfillment. Typically 5–50 employees, no in-house trade compliance team, relying on a customs broker who rarely raises drawback.

Pain Point

With tariff rates elevated across many product categories, duties are now a top-three cost line for these firms. Customs duty drawback lets them reclaim up to 99% of duties, taxes and fees paid on imported goods that are later exported or destroyed, within a 5-year window. Most eligible companies never file: the claim requires linking specific import entries to specific export records (often via 8-digit HTS "substitution" matching), keeping proof of export, and filing through ACE with precise data. Drawback specialists exist but typically take 20–30% of the refund, have minimum-size thresholds, and run slow, spreadsheet-heavy processes. The result is a large pile of unclaimed cash sitting in expiring 5-year windows, invisible to the firms that are owed it.

MVP Workflow

  1. User uploads (or emails in) their ACE entry-summary export from their broker (CSV/PDF) and an export ledger: commercial invoices, shipping records, or AES/EEI filings.
  2. The system parses entries and exports, normalizes part numbers and HTS codes, and computes a "refund opportunity" estimate within minutes, showing the dollar value by entry and the date each import window expires.
  3. A matching engine pairs imports to exports under the applicable drawback rules (direct identification or 8-digit HTS substitution), optimizing for the highest total refund and flagging entries near the 5-year cutoff first.
  4. For each proposed claim, the tool checks the required evidence (proof of export, proof of duty payment, no double-claiming, no prior liquidation problems) and lists precisely what is missing, with request templates the user can send to their freight forwarder or broker.
  5. Once complete, it generates the claim packet in the format needed for ACE filing (data file plus supporting-document index) for a licensed broker or the user's customs-filing partner to submit; a dashboard then tracks each claim through to refund receipt.

Data & Integrations Needed

ACE entry-summary reports (via broker export) Export records (AES/EEI, bills of lading, invoices) HTS schedule & duty-rate tables ERP/accounting exports (QuickBooks, NetSuite, Shopify) Document AI for invoices & shipping docs Customs broker / filer partner API or handoff

Pricing Model

Free opportunity scan (the lead magnet: "you have $84K in refundable duties, $31K expiring in 90 days"). After that, a success fee of 8–12% of refunds actually received — well below the 20–30% incumbents charge — or a $499/mo subscription with a capped fee for firms that file continuously. Success-based pricing makes the buy decision nearly risk-free.

First Version Scope

One narrow lane: manufacturing-drawback claims are out; v1 covers only "unused merchandise" and straight re-export of imported goods, where record-keeping is simplest. Manual CSV/PDF upload only, no live ACE integration. The v1 deliverable is the opportunity scan plus a matched, evidence-checked claim packet; filing itself is handed to a partner licensed customs broker who charges a flat fee per claim. Acquire users through customs-broker referrals, export-focused trade associations, state export-assistance programs, and LinkedIn/newsletter content aimed at importers reacting to tariff costs. Validate accuracy with two or three friendly pilot importers by comparing claims against what a specialist would file.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Drawback is an old, obscure program, and the industry around it was built for large importers, so small firms are simply below the radar — yet tariffs have turned the previously marginal refund into serious money for exactly those firms. The work is a matching and evidence-assembly problem across messy structured data, which is where automation genuinely beats a chatbot, and the free scan shows the customer a dollar figure of cash they didn't know they were owed. The 5-year expiry means every day of delay destroys refund value, giving the product a natural urgency and a built-in "expiring soon" trigger. Finally, a success-fee model undercuts the incumbents while aligning perfectly with the customer's incentives.
