SaaS Idea Log — 2026-10-13

BackflowBeat

Turns a backflow tester's field readings into the exact report format each water utility demands, files it, and chases every overdue customer before the utility threatens a shutoff.

Target Customer

Solo and small-crew certified backflow assembly testers (often plumbers or irrigation contractors with 1–5 techs) who test 500–5,000 devices a year for restaurants, apartment complexes, car washes, medical offices and irrigation systems across several water districts.

Pain Point

Every water utility requires annual testing of cross-connection devices, and each one has its own form, portal, email address, due-date rules and fee. Testers re-key the same gauge readings (check valve psi, relief valve opening point, pass/fail, repairs made) into paper forms, PDFs and clunky utility portals, then spend hours calling customers whose devices went overdue. Missed or malformed submissions mean the utility sends the customer a shutoff notice, the customer blames the tester, and the tester loses the account. Generic field-service software does not know any of this; the few incumbents are utility-side tools that testers are forced to use rather than choose.

MVP Workflow

  1. Tester imports their device list (spreadsheet or photos of old tags); AI extracts address, device type, size, serial, and the utility serving it.
  2. On site, the tester enters readings in a phone form (or snaps the gauge and test-kit display; vision model proposes values for confirmation) and captures a signature.
  3. BackflowBeat renders the report in the right utility template — PDF, CSV row, or portal-ready field set — and emails it to the utility's intake address, or presents a one-tap copy sheet for portal-only utilities.
  4. A due-date engine applies each utility's rules (anniversary vs. calendar-year, grace periods, retest windows after a failed repair) and auto-texts customers at 60/30/7 days with a booking link.
  5. A weekly digest shows which filings the utility has acknowledged, which are rejected (with the reason parsed from reply emails), and which customers are at risk of a shutoff notice.

Data & Integrations Needed

Per-utility report templates & rules (crowd-built library) Vision/OCR on gauge & tag photos Email in/out parsing for utility replies SMS (Twilio) Calendar sync QuickBooks / Stripe invoicing State tester-certification expiry tracking

Pricing Model

$49/month per tester including 100 filings, then $0.60 per additional filing. Optional $99/month "utility library plus" add-on for adding custom utility templates within 48 hours. A free tier for 10 filings per month seeds word of mouth among testers who share a supply house.

First Version Scope

One state and its ten largest water districts (pick the state with the most fragmented utilities). Support only reduced-pressure, double-check and pressure-vacuum assemblies. Email-based filing and PDF generation only; portal-only utilities get the copy-sheet fallback. Manual entry of readings in v1; photo reading is v1.1. Acquire users through testers' state association meetings, backflow-certification classes, and plumbing supply counters.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The buyer isn't the utility or the building owner but the tester, a tiny tradesperson underserved by software because the market looks like a thousand different local rule sets. That fragmentation is the moat: each utility template and rule set added makes the product more valuable to every other tester in the area, and it is tedious enough that big field-service vendors won't build it. The automation is not a chat interface but quiet back-office work — reading a utility's rejection email and turning it into a fix — and the pain is concrete: a lost account over a paperwork error is real money, so willingness to pay is easy to validate with a handful of calls.
