SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-09

ReserveSentry

AI reads a condo or HOA's structural reserve study and engineering reports, maps them against each state's post-Surfside inspection and funding deadlines, and auto-drafts the board disclosures before the fines and liability hit.

Target Customer

Community association management (CAM) companies and independent community managers who administer 10–200+ condo and HOA boards, often spread across multiple states, each now subject to a different structural integrity reserve study (SIRS) and milestone-inspection law.

Pain Point

Since the 2021 Champlain Towers South collapse, states have raced to pass structural reserve and inspection laws — Florida's SB 4-D Milestone Inspection and SIRS, California's SB326/SB721 balcony inspection law, Colorado's HB21-1229, and similar statutes now moving through Virginia, Illinois, and Georgia — each with different building-age and height thresholds, inspection intervals, reserve-funding percentages, and owner-disclosure deadlines. A manager running dozens of associations across a few states must correctly identify which law applies to which building, translate a 40–80 page engineer's PDF into the exact numeric disclosures each state requires be sent to owners, and refile on a schedule no two states share — with fines, personal board-member liability, and insurer non-renewal as the downside of a missed date. Today this lives in a spreadsheet and a calendar reminder per property, and a single missed Milestone Inspection filing can get a county to compel a special assessment or restrict occupancy.

MVP Workflow

  1. Manager uploads each association's engineering/reserve study PDF along with the building's state, county, year built, and number of stories.
  2. System identifies which state and local statutes apply and generates that property's full compliance calendar — every inspection, filing, and owner-disclosure deadline.
  3. Document AI extracts the reserve study's key figures: component list, remaining useful life per component, percent funded, and recommended annual contribution.
  4. System auto-drafts the state-mandated board disclosure and reserve-funding notice, pre-filled with the extracted figures, ready for board sign-off and owner mailing.
  5. A portfolio dashboard shows every property's compliance status (green/yellow/red) with email/SMS alerts as deadlines approach, and one-click regeneration whenever a new reserve study is uploaded.
  6. Any property with a missing filing or a reserve study past its mandated re-inspection interval is flagged as an active liability risk needing immediate board attention.

Data & Integrations Needed

Curated state/local statute database (FL SB 4-D, CA SB326/SB721, CO HB21-1229, VA, IL, GA) Document AI for engineering & reserve study PDF parsing Deadline/compliance-calendar engine with email/SMS alerts E-signature workflow for board approval Optional sync with CAM platforms (Vantaca, TOPS, AppFolio)

Pricing Model

Per-property SaaS subscription sold to CAM firms — roughly $99–$299/month per property depending on unit count, billed against the same per-door software budget CAMs already carry — plus an optional annual "compliance audit" add-on where a licensed engineering partner reviews any property flagged high-risk.

First Version Scope

Cover Florida only for v1 — SB 4-D's Milestone Inspection and SIRS requirements are the most urgent, best-documented, and highest-stakes, with the largest existing base of CAM firms exposed to them. Expand to California and Colorado in v1.1. Ship the compliance calendar, document parsing, and auto-drafted disclosures first; skip full e-signature and defer to PDF export for manual board sign-off. Go to market through Florida CAI chapters, county building-department mailing lists, and condo law firms who already field compliance questions from clients.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Everyone assumed the post-Surfside SIRS wave created a market for engineering firms doing the physical inspections — but the chronic, recurring pain is downstream of that: translating one engineer's idiosyncratic report into a fast-multiplying, inconsistent patchwork of state-specific disclosure and filing obligations, repeated across dozens of properties per manager, on a calendar no two states share. Because these laws are new, still being amended annually, and legally distinct per jurisdiction, no incumbent CAM platform — built around accounting and resident portals — has rebuilt itself around them, leaving a multi-year window before this becomes commodity. And the buyer is the CAM firm managing many boards at once, not the underfunded volunteer board itself, so the willingness-to-pay is professional-services-grade even though the end beneficiary would never buy software on its own.
