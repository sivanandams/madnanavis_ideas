SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-05

PayDirt

AI reconciles an oil & gas royalty owner's monthly check stubs against public commodity price indexes, state severance tax tables, and their own lease language — catching underpayment that boutique audit firms won't touch because the account is too small.

Target Customer

Non-operating mineral and royalty interest owners in U.S. oil & gas basins (Permian, Eagle Ford, Bakken, Marcellus/Appalachia, Anadarko/SCOOP-STACK) — mostly people who inherited mineral rights or leased family land a generation ago and now receive monthly or quarterly check-stub statements from an operator, typically earning $2,000–$75,000/year in royalty income. Large enough to care, far too small for a contingency-fee forensic audit firm to bother with.

Pain Point

Royalty check stubs are dense, operator-specific, and deliberately hard to audit: gross value, wellhead price, "post-production" deductions (gathering, compression, dehydration, transportation, processing), severance tax, and the owner's decimal interest are crammed into a cryptic line-item format that differs by operator and changes without notice. State law and the owner's own lease often restrict which deductions are even allowed — some leases are "cost-free" to the royalty owner — but almost no one checks, because doing so means manually cross-referencing the stub against lease clauses, state statutes, and the actual NYMEX/regional index price for that production month. Industry studies and state lawsuits have repeatedly found systematic underpayment through disallowed deductions, below-index pricing, and stale decimal interests after ownership transfers. Forensic royalty auditors exist, but they work on contingency and only take owners with large enough interests to justify the effort — leaving the long tail of small owners with no economical way to know if they're being shorted, so most just cash the check.

MVP Workflow

  1. Owner uploads recent check-stub PDFs (or forwards the operator's statement email) plus their lease and, if available, the division order showing their decimal interest.
  2. Document AI extracts, per production month and well: gross value, volume, wellhead/index price used, each deduction category and amount, severance tax withheld, and net paid.
  3. An NLP pass over the lease flags the governing deduction language (e.g., "free of cost to lessor," a specific post-production cost clause, or silence — which defaults to state common law and varies by jurisdiction) and pulls the recorded decimal interest for comparison.
  4. The system cross-checks the stub's price against the public regional index (Henry Hub, WTI Midland/Cushing, or the relevant basin differential) for that production month, checks severance tax against the state's published rate, and flags: below-index pricing without a stated quality/transport reason, deductions the lease doesn't authorize, and decimal-interest mismatches versus recorded ownership.
  5. Owner gets a plain-language discrepancy report with an estimated dollar-per-line impact, annualized, plus a generated inquiry/demand letter citing the specific lease clause or state statute (e.g., Texas Natural Resources Code §91.501 prompt-payment rules, Kansas or Oklahoma proceeds-from-production statutes) ready to send to the operator's division order department.
  6. If nothing is off, the report says so — a fast, credible "you're being paid correctly" answer is itself valuable, since most owners currently have zero way to know either way.

Data & Integrations Needed

Document AI for check-stub & lease parsing Public commodity index feeds (Henry Hub, WTI, EIA basin differentials) State severance tax rate tables Corpus of state royalty-payment statutes & deduction case law Optional county clerk / GLO deed & lease record lookups for decimal-interest verification Email-forwarding ingestion for recurring stubs

Pricing Model

Flat one-time audit fee of $199–$399 covering up to 24 months of stubs for a well/lease. A $19–$39/mo "royalty watch" subscription auto-ingests new monthly stubs via email forwarding and flags anomalies going forward without re-uploading. For flagged underpayments above a threshold (say $5,000), an optional warm handoff to an affiliate royalty-litigation attorney network on a referral-fee basis — PayDirt stays software-only and never takes contingency itself.

First Version Scope

Cover oil and natural gas royalty statements only, limited to Texas, Oklahoma, and North Dakota — the three states with the clearest statutory deduction rules and the largest population of small non-operating owners. Skip working-interest/joint-interest billing statements and skip wind/solar lease audits entirely for v1. Manual PDF upload only; email-forwarding automation ships as a v1.1 retention feature once parsing accuracy is proven. Go to market through NARO (National Association of Royalty Owners) state chapters, mineral-rights Facebook groups, and forums like the r/mineralrights community and MineralAnswers.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The existing royalty-audit market is entirely contingency-fee boutique firms that cherry-pick large working-interest accounts, which means the millions of small non-operating owners — the exact people a $299 flat-fee SaaS tool can serve profitably — are systematically ignored by the current supply of auditors, not fought over by them. The core value is also deliberately non-conversational: it's a deterministic reconciliation against public price indices, published tax tables, and statutory text, producing a verifiable, line-itemized discrepancy trail rather than a summarized chat answer — which makes it far more defensible against a generic LLM wrapper than most "AI audits your documents" pitches. And because royalty stubs arrive monthly for the life of the well, a one-time audit naturally upsells into a recurring "statement immune system," giving the product a built-in retention hook that most one-and-done compliance tools lack.
