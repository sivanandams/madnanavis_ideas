LienGuard

AI turns a delinquent self-storage tenant's file into a state-exact lien notice, mail-and-publication timeline, and auction-ready compliance binder — so independent facility owners stop having their unit auctions voided (or getting sued) over a missed statutory step.

Target Customer

Independent and small-chain self-storage facility owners and the third-party management companies that run 1–25 non-REIT locations (the long tail below Public Storage, Extra Space, and CubeSmart, who all run in-house legal). These operators typically use general-purpose storage software (SiteLink, StorEdge, Storable) for billing and access control, but handle lien/auction paperwork manually or off inherited templates.

Pain Point

Every state's Self-Storage Facility Act (or equivalent) prescribes an exact sequence before a facility can legally sell a delinquent tenant's belongings: a default waiting period, a certified-letter lien notice with specific mandatory language and itemized charges, a second waiting period, a published or online auction notice with its own required content, and a minimum gap before the sale date. Miss any element — wrong notice period, missing statutory phrase, mailing one day early — and the sale can be ruled a "wrongful lien sale," voiding the auction and exposing the facility to tenant lawsuits that in several states carry treble damages and attorney's fees. Property managers are usually non-lawyers with high turnover, working from a binder of templates that quietly goes stale every time a state legislature amends the law (which happens most years somewhere), so the failure is invisible until a tenant's attorney finds the gap — often during litigation, when it's too late to fix.

MVP Workflow

  1. Manager uploads (or syncs via CSV export from SiteLink/StorEdge) the delinquent-tenant list: unit, balance owed, last payment date, tenant contact info, and facility state.
  2. Engine calculates the state-specific statutory timeline per unit — default maturity date, lien-notice mail-by date, minimum waiting period, publication deadline, earliest legal auction date — shown as a live per-unit countdown dashboard.
  3. AI drafts the state-compliant lien notice with the exact required statutory language and itemized fees pre-filled, and generates a matching certified-mail merge (via a mail API) plus the published/online-auction notice text.
  4. Before the sale is scheduled, a compliance check compares actual mail-send dates and elapsed days against the statute and flags any deviation — wrong wait period, missing clause, early posting — so the manager can correct course before auctioning.
  5. On completion, the system outputs a timestamped "auction-ready" compliance binder (PDF) documenting every notice, date, and delivery confirmation — the exact evidence a facility needs if a tenant later disputes the sale.

Data & Integrations Needed

50-state self-storage lien statute library (maintained + versioned) Storage management software exports (SiteLink, StorEdge, Storable) Certified mail / delivery-confirmation API (e.g., Lob) Online auction listing sites (StorageTreasures, Lockerfox) for notice cross-posting PDF generation & document-timestamping Statute change-monitoring feed

Pricing Model

Per-facility SaaS subscription, $99–$179/mo per location covering unlimited active lien cases, plus a small per-letter pass-through fee for certified mail. Management companies running multiple locations get a per-portfolio volume discount. No usage-based case fee, since predictable monthly cost is what a small operator budgets for legal-risk tooling.

First Version Scope

Launch with hand-verified statute templates for the ten states with the highest self-storage facility density (TX, CA, FL, GA, NC, OH, IL, PA, TN, AZ). Manual CSV upload only for v1 — skip direct PMS API integration. Ship the countdown dashboard, notice drafting, certified-mail merge, and compliance check; leave automated cross-posting to auction sites as a v1.1 feature (v1 generates the notice text for manual copy-paste instead). Go to market through state chapters of the Self Storage Association, storage operator Facebook/LinkedIn groups, and the app marketplaces of SiteLink and Storable.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Everyone assumes the storage management software already covers this, because SiteLink and StorEdge do generate "lien letters" — but those are static templates for tenant billing workflows, not statute-tracking engines, and they don't get updated the moment a state legislature amends its self-storage act. The gap is invisible until a tenant's lawyer finds it during a wrongful-sale claim, which is exactly why nobody builds for it: the pain is deferred and probabilistic, right up until it's a five-figure legal bill. That deferred-but-catastrophic failure mode is also what makes this a genuinely good fit for structured AI rather than a chatbot: the value isn't "answer my legal question," it's continuously maintaining and applying a versioned, jurisdiction-specific rules engine against a tenant timeline — a compliance-automation problem, not a conversation. And because the statute library and mail-audit trail are the actual product, the moat compounds with every legislative session a competitor hasn't tracked yet.
