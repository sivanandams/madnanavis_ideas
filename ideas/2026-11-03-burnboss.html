BurnBoss

Burn-window forecasting and auto-generated prescribed-burn plans for contract burn crews and landowners, so one good weather day turns into a permitted, documented, insured burn instead of a missed season.

Target Customer

Independent contract prescribed-burn businesses (1–10 person crews serving ranchers, timber owners, hunting-land owners and conservation groups), plus prescribed burn associations and consulting foresters in the US Southeast, Texas/Oklahoma, and the Pacific Northwest where state "certified burn manager" programs are expanding.

Pain Point

Prescribed burns are legal only inside a narrow envelope of wind speed/direction, relative humidity, fuel moisture, mixing height, smoke-sensitive receptors and county burn bans. A crew might get 8–15 usable days a year per client, and each burn needs a written burn plan, a permit or notification, a go/no-go checklist and an after-action record to keep liability coverage and certified-burn-manager protections. Today that means juggling NWS spot forecasts, state forestry websites, phone calls and Word templates the night before — then frequently discovering at 7 a.m. the window closed. Missed windows mean lost revenue for the crew and overgrown, wildfire-prone land for the client.

MVP Workflow

  1. Crew draws each client unit on a map and answers a short form: fuel type, slope, target objectives, nearby roads/homes/smoke-sensitive sites, and burn-boss prescription limits (e.g., wind 5–12 mph from SW, RH 30–55%).
  2. BurnBoss scores every unit against the 7-day forecast and local fuel-moisture observations, then texts the crew a ranked list: "Unit 14 is a GO Thursday 11:00–16:00; Unit 9 fails on RH after 2 p.m."
  3. On a chosen day it pre-fills the state's burn notification/permit data and checks active burn bans or air-quality restrictions for the county.
  4. It generates the burn plan PDF (map, prescription, ignition pattern suggestion, holding resources, contingency and smoke-impact notes) from the unit data and the forecast.
  5. On burn day, a phone checklist logs spot weather readings against prescription and issues a live go/no-go flag; afterward it auto-builds the after-action record and client invoice line item.

Data & Integrations Needed

NWS gridded & spot forecasts (free API) RAWS / MesoWest fuel & weather observations NOAA HRRR-Smoke dispersion output State forestry burn-ban & permit pages Parcel/map layers (county GIS, OpenStreetMap) SMS (Twilio) & PDF generation

Pricing Model

$59/mo per crew for up to 25 units, $149/mo for burn associations managing shared member units and equipment, plus an optional $15 per generated burn plan for landowners without a crew subscription. Crews recoup the fee on their first rescued burn day.

First Version Scope

Launch in two or three states with the largest contract-burner population and the most uniform notification process (e.g., Georgia, Florida, Texas). Ship unit mapping, prescription-vs-forecast scoring, SMS go/no-go alerts, and a templated burn-plan PDF. Skip live permit submission (link out to the state portal, with pre-filled fields to copy) and advanced smoke modeling in v1. Go to market through prescribed burn association meetings, state certified-burn-manager course rosters, and extension-service newsletters.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Wildfire tech money chases detection and suppression, but the preventive tool — prescribed fire — is run by tiny crews on spreadsheets and text threads, and policy tailwinds (new burn-manager certification and liability-protection laws) are pushing more landowners to hire them. The product is not a chatbot: it is a prescription-matching engine over public weather data, where the value is a correct, timely "go" on the one day that works. Because burn plans and after-action logs double as liability and insurance evidence, the records become a switching-cost moat the crews would otherwise never build themselves.
