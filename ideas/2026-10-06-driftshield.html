DriftShield

AI turns a spray applicator's job tickets and public weather history into a timestamped, label-compliance "defense file" — so a pesticide drift complaint is answered with proof in minutes instead of a five-figure settlement.

Target Customer

Independent custom pesticide and herbicide applicators — small aerial (ag-aviation) and ground-rig spray businesses with 1–15 trucks or aircraft, typically serving row-crop and specialty-crop farmers in the US Midwest, Delta and California Central Valley.

Pain Point

When a neighbor's soybeans, vineyard or beehives show damage, the applicator is the first person blamed for "drift" — and the state agriculture department can demand, within days, restricted-use pesticide (RUP) records, wind speed and direction at time of application, label compliance, buffer-zone evidence and applicator licensing proof. Applicators currently keep this on carbon-copy tickets, GPS flight logs and memory. A single drift claim means five-figure settlements, insurance premium spikes or license action, and most are decided on whose paperwork is more complete — not who was actually at fault. Existing farm-management software is built for growers, not for the contractor on the hook.

MVP Workflow

  1. Applicator snaps a photo of the job ticket (or forwards the dispatcher's text); document AI extracts field, product, EPA reg. number, rate, acreage and times.
  2. The system pulls hourly historical wind, temperature-inversion indicators and humidity for the field's coordinates from public weather data, and checks the product label's restrictions (max wind, temperature, buffers, sensitive-crop setbacks) against those conditions.
  3. Each job gets a locked, timestamped "defense file": ticket, weather snapshot, label-compliance check, nearby sensitive sites (apiaries, organic farms, schools, waterways) and flight/GPS track if uploaded.
  4. Pre-spray, the app flags a job whose forecast window violates the label so the dispatcher can reschedule before the problem exists.
  5. When a complaint arrives, one click assembles the state-formatted record packet and a plain-language timeline for the department of agriculture and the insurer.
  6. Monthly and annual RUP reports export in the state's required layout.

NOAA / state mesonet weather historyEPA pesticide label database (PPLS)FieldWatch / DriftWatch sensitive-site registriesDocument AI for job ticketsGPS track import (AgNav, Satloc, GPX)State RUP report templates

Pricing Model

$79/month per truck or aircraft with unlimited jobs, plus a $249 one-off "complaint packet" export for operators on a basic plan. Insurance-channel angle: agricultural aviation underwriters may subsidize or discount premiums for customers with defense files, giving a second revenue path through broker partnerships.

First Version Scope

Two states only (e.g. Iowa and Arkansas), ground and aerial rigs, row crops. Photo-of-ticket capture, label-vs-weather check and the defense-file PDF. No live telemetry integration, no grower portal, no chemical inventory. Go to market through state aerial applicator association meetings, ag-aviation insurance brokers and custom-applicator Facebook groups.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The buyer is not the farmer — the crowded part of ag-tech — but the contractor who carries the liability and has almost no software. The product's value is proof that is created at the moment of work, not reconstructed after a complaint, so it is a defensive asset that gets more valuable the one time a year it matters. Public weather history and label restrictions are free but scattered; joining them to a specific ticket is a small, tractable automation problem rather than a chatbot, and an insurer-backed distribution channel is available that no horizontal tool can reach.
