SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-23

CaskCompliance

AI-assisted interstate alcohol compliance and distributor-franchise tracking for craft breweries and distilleries expanding beyond their home state.

Target Customer

Craft breweries and distilleries doing roughly $2M–$20M in annual revenue that distribute in 3–15 states and have no in-house compliance or legal staff — typically a founder, an operations manager, and an outside law firm they call only when something's already on fire.

Pain Point

The US three-tier alcohol system means every state has its own licensing renewal cycles, label registration (COLA) rules, tied-house restrictions, and — critically — franchise/termination laws that can trap a brewery in a bad distributor relationship indefinitely once signed. Missing a license renewal or violating a franchise-law notice period can mean losing distribution rights in a state entirely, sometimes for years, or facing fines that dwarf the cost of prevention. Today this is tracked in spreadsheets, sticky notes, and outside counsel billed at $300–$600/hour — counsel that gets called reactively, after a distributor has already sent a termination notice or a state has already flagged a lapsed permit, not proactively before the deadline.

MVP Workflow

  1. Brewery onboards by listing the states it currently distributes in and uploads its distributor agreements for each.
  2. Document AI extracts key terms from each contract — territory, term length, renewal date, and the specific notice period and "cause" language required to terminate under that state's franchise law.
  3. A human-curated, AI-summarized feed of state ABC (Alcohol Beverage Control) board bulletins flags relevant regulatory changes and upcoming license renewal windows for each state the brewery operates in.
  4. A dashboard surfaces every upcoming deadline — license renewals, label registrations, distributor agreement anniversaries — ranked by days remaining and dollar/legal risk.
  5. Automated email/Slack alerts fire on a graduated schedule (90/30/7 days) before each deadline.
  6. When the brewery wants to enter a new state, the system generates an AI-drafted compliance checklist (licensing steps, franchise-law summary, tied-house rules) pulled from the same underlying dataset.

Data & Integrations Needed

State ABC board bulletins/websites TTB COLA label database Document AI for contract extraction State franchise-law reference corpus Email/Slack alerting Calendar sync (Google/Outlook)

Pricing Model

Tiered SaaS by number of states monitored: Starter $299/mo (up to 5 states), Growth $799/mo (up to 15 states), Enterprise custom for multi-state distribution portfolios. A one-time $500 "new-state entry report" add-on for breweries expanding into a state they don't yet monitor.

First Version Scope

Cover the ten states with the highest craft beer distribution volume (CA, TX, FL, NY, CO, WA, OR, IL, PA, NC) with human-curated regulatory monitoring plus AI summarization, rather than fully automated scraping of all 50 states. Ship contract upload with AI term extraction and the deadline dashboard/alerts first; the new-state-entry checklist generator can follow once the core tracking is validated with design partners. Go to market through the Brewers Association, state craft brewers guilds, and alcohol-beverage attorneys who'd rather refer routine deadline tracking than bill hourly for it.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Most compliance SaaS chases the well-trodden, well-funded categories — HIPAA, SOC 2, GDPR — where incumbents already dominate. Alcohol beverage compliance is a real, expensive, legally fragmented pain (10,000+ US craft breweries and distilleries, each facing a different rulebook per state) that stays underserved precisely because it looks too narrow and too weird for generalist compliance vendors to bother with, and generic legal-AI chatbots have no structured advantage here — the value is in extracting hard dates and franchise-law triggers from real contracts and regulatory text, not in conversational Q&A. The wedge is also naturally viral within a tight professional community: alcohol-beverage attorneys who currently bill for deadline-tracking would rather hand it off to a tool and refer clients to it than keep doing it themselves at their hourly rate.
