BatchClear

AI reads every incoming Certificate of Analysis from a supplement or nutraceutical brand's contract manufacturer, checks it against FDA limits and the brand's own label claims, and issues an auditable batch-release decision before inventory sits idle in a warehouse.

Target Customer

The founder, ops lead, or lone quality/regulatory person at small-to-mid dietary supplement and nutraceutical brands ($1M–$50M revenue) who contract-manufacture their products and are legally the "manufacturer of record" responsible for batch release under FDA 21 CFR 111 — even though they typically have no dedicated QA department.

Pain Point

Before a batch can ship or list on Amazon, someone at the brand has to review the contract manufacturer's Certificate of Analysis (CoA) — identity, potency, heavy metals, microbials — against FDA action limits and the brand's own label claims, and formally approve release. At small brands this means a non-QA person eyeballing a lab PDF in Gmail, often under time pressure because the batch is sitting in paid warehouse storage until it's released. Missed contamination flags trigger recalls and FDA warning letters; missed label-claim mismatches trigger Amazon CoA-audit suspensions that can wipe out a listing overnight. There's no dedicated tool for this specific, recurring, safety-critical review — brands either do it manually and inconsistently or pay a compliance consultant a retainer to do the same manual review, slower.

MVP Workflow

  1. Brand points its contract manufacturer to a dedicated inbox/upload portal for batch CoAs and label copy, per SKU.
  2. Document AI extracts every CoA line item (test, method, result, unit, limit), OCR'ing scanned or handwritten lab results where needed.
  3. A rules engine checks each result against configured specs: FDA 21 CFR 111 action limits for heavy metals/microbials, the brand's own potency claim range pulled from the label, and any retailer-specific requirement (e.g., Amazon's heavy-metal thresholds for certain categories).
  4. System returns a pass / fail / flag-for-human-review verdict per batch, with the offending line item highlighted directly on the CoA.
  5. A signed batch-release memo is generated automatically as the audit record required for FDA inspection or Amazon appeal.
  6. A dashboard tracks batches awaiting release, average release turnaround time, and which manufacturers/labs generate the most flags — useful leverage in CMO renegotiations.

Data & Integrations Needed

Email/PDF ingestion & document AI FDA 21 CFR 111 & Part 101 limit tables Amazon/retailer CoA requirement rulesets Per-SKU spec & label-claim configuration Optional 3PL/inventory integration (ShipBob, Extensiv) for auto hold/release

Pricing Model

Per-SKU monthly subscription (roughly $49/SKU/mo with volume tiers) since the workload scales with active SKUs and batch frequency, not company headcount. An optional audit-ready compliance binder export add-on ($99/mo) serves brands preparing for FDA inspection or an Amazon seller-performance review.

First Version Scope

Dietary supplements only for v1 (defer cosmetics and food). Support extraction from the CoA templates used by the dozen or so most common contract manufacturers via few-shot examples, with manual spec configuration only — no live retailer API integration yet. Go to market through supplement-brand Slack and Facebook founder communities, and by partnering directly with CMOs, who benefit when their brand clients release batches (and pay CMO invoices) faster.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Most "compliance software" pitches aim at the manufacturer's QA system, but the actual liability and bottleneck sit on the brand side, with untrained staff approving safety-critical documents by eye in their inbox. Because release approval is required on every single batch — not a one-time audit — this bakes recurring, non-discretionary usage directly into the brand's physical inventory flow, which is a stronger retention mechanic than typical compliance-checklist SaaS. And the contract manufacturers themselves have a commercial incentive to push the tool onto their brand clients, since faster release approval means faster invoice payment for the CMO — creating a two-sided referral loop that's unusual in this category.
