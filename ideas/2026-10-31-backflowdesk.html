BackflowDesk

Turns a certified backflow tester's field readings into correctly formatted compliance reports, files each one with the right water utility's portal, and chases every device's annual re-test due date, so a one-person testing shop stops losing evenings to paperwork.

Target Customer

Solo and small-crew certified backflow assembly testers (often plumbers or irrigation contractors with an ASSE 5110 / state tester certification) who test 500–5,000 devices a year across several water districts and cities, plus the small property-management and HOA firms that hire them.

Pain Point

Almost every water utility requires an annual test on commercial backflow preventers, a certified tester's report, and timely submission. But each utility has its own form, its own portal (or fax and email), its own failure-and-retest rules, and its own deadline grace period. A tester finishes a day of crawling through vaults, then spends evenings re-typing the same gauge readings into four portals. Missed submissions mean the customer gets a shut-off notice and blames the tester. Tracking which of 2,000 devices is due when lives in a spreadsheet or a shoebox. Testers know it is the worst part of the job, and they already pay for tools that are generic field-service software and do not know any utility's rules.

MVP Workflow

  1. Tester imports existing device lists (CSV or a photo of the utility's due-list letter, parsed by document AI) to build a device registry with location, size, model, serial, hazard type and utility.
  2. On site, a phone-first form takes the test readings (check valve pressure drops, relief valve opening point, pass/fail, repairs made). A photo of the device tag auto-fills make, model and serial through OCR.
  3. The system validates readings against the device type's pass criteria and the utility's own rules, and flags a failure with the repair-and-retest deadline.
  4. It generates the utility-specific report as a PDF and submits it: through the utility's portal by browser automation where there is no API, or by emailing the exact form the utility accepts. A receipt is stored against the device.
  5. A due-date engine texts the customer 60/30/7 days ahead and offers a one-tap booking link, so the next year's route fills itself.
  6. Unsubmitted or rejected reports show on a single "owed to utilities" screen so nothing silently lapses.

Data & Integrations Needed

Per-utility form templates & rules library Utility submission portals (browser automation) Device-tag OCR Document AI for due-list letters SMS / email reminders QuickBooks invoicing export Tester certification & gauge calibration expiry tracking

Pricing Model

$49/mo per tester seat including 100 submitted reports, then $0.60 per additional report. A $99/mo shop tier adds crew accounts, customer reminders with booking links and QuickBooks sync. Because a single tester typically bills $60–$120 per test, saving even 10 minutes per report pays for the plan within a week.

First Version Scope

Launch in one metro area and cover its 5–8 largest water utilities, and hand-build each utility's template and submission path (no general-purpose portal automation yet). Ship device registry, mobile test form, PDF generation, email-based submission and the due-date reminders first. Portal automation is added utility by utility, ordered by how many early users file with them. Skip cross-connection surveys, fire-line assemblies and multi-state certification in v1. Go to market through state backflow-tester associations, local water district tester lists, and the Facebook and trade forums where testers already trade tips.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The value is not the form builder; it is the maintained library of local utility rules and submission quirks, which is boring, regional and exactly the thing a big field-service vendor will never bother to keep current. That library becomes a moat as it grows, and because every utility is a distinct integration, each new one both unlocks a new set of testers and raises the cost of switching away. Demand is also legally mandated and recurring: the same devices must be re-tested every year, so the due-date registry compounds into a predictable annual revenue base for both the tester and the product. AI is used where it earns its keep (reading tags and due-list letters, parsing rule changes) and never as a chat window.
