FleetTrace

AI cross-checks Amazon DSP settlement statements against your own route, stop, and delivery-photo data to catch underpaid routes and wrongful chargebacks — recovering the margin Amazon's black-box payouts quietly take back.

Target Customer

Owners of small Amazon Delivery Service Partner (DSP) companies — typically 20 to 60 vans per station — who are contractually required to run Amazon's dispatch and telematics stack but get paid via a weekly settlement statement bundling route pay with dozens of itemized deductions.

Pain Point

DSPs operate on notoriously thin net margins, often cited in the single digits, and Amazon's weekly settlement report mixes per-route pay with chargebacks for "package not received," customer escalations, safety-camera events, and vehicle-damage claims — most auto-generated from Amazon's own systems with no human review unless the DSP pushes back. Disputing a line item requires citing specific evidence (GPS ping, geofence, proof-of-delivery photo, timestamp) inside a narrow appeal window, typically 30 days, and reconciling that against 200+ routes a week is a full-time job nobody at a 30-van DSP has time for. The result: DSP owners routinely eat deductions for events their own delivery data would clearly refute, because nobody has stitched the evidence together before the window closes.

MVP Workflow

  1. DSP owner connects their weekly settlement export (CSV/PDF from the Amazon Relay/DSP portal) and their internal dispatch export — stop list, GPS log, and proof-of-delivery photos — from whatever TMS they already run (Relay, Detrack, Onfleet).
  2. The system line-item-matches every deduction and chargeback against the corresponding stop's timestamp, geofence position, and delivery photo.
  3. An anomaly engine flags deductions where the evidence contradicts Amazon's stated reason — e.g. a "package not received" charge on a stop with a POD photo and an in-geofence GPS ping at the delivery time.
  4. For each flagged item, the system auto-drafts a dispute formatted for Amazon's DSP chargeback appeal process, attaching the specific photo, GPS, and timestamp evidence and citing the settlement line-item ID.
  5. A weekly digest shows total dollars disputed, the running win rate on submitted disputes, and a cumulative "recovered margin" tally the owner can hand straight to their accountant.

Data & Integrations Needed

Amazon DSP portal / Relay settlement exports TMS/dispatch exports (Detrack, Onfleet, Relay) GPS & telematics logs (Netradyne, Samsara) OCR/PDF parsing for settlement statements Geofence + timestamp evidence matching Dispute-submission templating for Amazon's appeal workflow

Pricing Model

Success fee of 20% of every deduction disputed and won — no monthly cost, since DSP owners are wary of adding fixed expenses on razor-thin margins. An optional $99/mo flat tier suits larger DSPs (50+ vans) that want unlimited disputes filed without per-recovery fees eating into high-volume weeks.

First Version Scope

Cover the two or three highest-frequency, highest-dollar deduction categories first — "package not received" and safety-event chargebacks — before expanding to rarer categories. Manual CSV/PDF upload only, since Amazon offers no settlement API. Recruit a pilot cohort station-by-station through DSP owner Facebook groups and forums rather than broad marketing, validating dispute win-rate accuracy before scaling to more stations.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The pain isn't fraud by Amazon — it's that any large platform's automated deduction system produces a real error rate at scale that nobody reviews unless the counterparty pushes back with structured evidence, and the evidence required is exactly what Amazon itself already mandates DSPs capture (GPS, photo, timestamp) but has never handed back to them in a reconciled form outside its own opaque portal. That means the product needs no cooperation or data access from Amazon at all — only the DSP's own already-exported records — which keeps it buildable by a solo founder. And because there are only a few thousand DSPs nationally, each running a business-defining thin margin, this is small and well-defined enough to sell station-by-station inside existing DSP owner communities rather than needing broad brand spend.
