MACBack

AI matches an independent pharmacy's PBM remittances against its own wholesaler invoices and NADAC benchmarks, flags every below-cost reimbursement, and files the MAC price appeal before the short appeal window closes.

Target Customer

Independent community pharmacies (roughly 18,000 in the US, typically 1–3 locations) with an owner-pharmacist who also runs the back office, and the small pharmacy services administrative organizations (PSAOs) that serve them.

Pain Point

PBMs reimburse generics using proprietary Maximum Allowable Cost (MAC) lists that change constantly and are often below what the pharmacy paid its wholesaler, so many dispenses lose money. Most states give pharmacies a right to appeal a MAC price, but the window is short (often 7–14 days from the claim), requires the NDC, invoice proof, and a wholesaler availability showing, and is filed through a different portal for each PBM. Owners know they are "getting killed on generics" but can't prove it claim by claim; the appeal work is tedious enough that only a small fraction of eligible claims is ever appealed, and the money is simply lost.

MVP Workflow

  1. Pharmacy connects its pharmacy management system export (daily claims file) and uploads PBM 835 remittance files and wholesaler invoice feeds.
  2. The system joins each paid claim to the most recent purchase invoice for that NDC and computes true margin per claim, comparing against the NADAC survey price for context.
  3. Claims reimbursed below acquisition cost are queued as appeal candidates, ranked by dollars recovered and by days left in that PBM's appeal window (rules table per PBM and per state).
  4. For each candidate, the system assembles the appeal packet: invoice excerpt, NDC, wholesaler availability evidence, and the state statute citation, in the format each PBM portal or fax form expects.
  5. Pharmacist reviews a one-screen batch and approves; the system submits (portal automation or pre-filled PDF/email) and tracks outcomes.
  6. Weekly report shows recovered dollars, appeal win rate per PBM, and drugs where the pharmacy should stop dispensing or switch wholesaler/NDC.

Data & Integrations Needed

NCPDP 835 remittance files Pharmacy management system exports (PioneerRx, Rx30, QS/1 CSV) Wholesaler invoices (McKesson, Cardinal, AmerisourceBergen, via EDI/CSV) CMS NADAC weekly file (public) State MAC appeal statutes & PBM rules table Deadline engine

Pricing Model

$199/month per location with a success-fee alternative of 10% of recovered dollars. Typical independent pharmacies see five-figure annual below-cost reimbursement, so the subscription pays for itself with a handful of won appeals. PSAO white-label tier at a per-pharmacy wholesale rate.

First Version Scope

One pharmacy management system export format, one wholesaler invoice format, and the three largest PBMs in two launch states with strong MAC appeal statutes. Batch CSV upload only, no live integrations. Output is a ranked appeal-candidate list with pre-filled packets that the pharmacist submits manually; automated submission and outcome tracking come in v1.1. Go to market through state independent pharmacy associations and pharmacy owner Facebook groups.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Existing pharmacy software tracks reimbursement in aggregate or audits the pharmacy for the PBM; almost nothing works claim-by-claim on the pharmacy's side with a deadline-driven appeal workflow. The moat is an unglamorous rules table (appeal windows, portals, evidence requirements per PBM and state) plus accumulated win-rate data that tells each pharmacy which appeals are worth filing. The value is measured in recovered dollars, so ROI is provable in the first month, and the buyer is an owner-operator who feels the pain every day. AI is used for document matching and packet assembly, not conversation.
