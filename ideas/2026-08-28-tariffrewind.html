SaaS Idea Log — 2026-08-28

TariffRewind

AI re-classifies your customs entries against CBP rulings and the tariff schedule, flags duty you overpaid, and files the correction before your refund window closes.

Target Customer

Small-to-mid U.S. importers ($5M–$150M revenue) — e-commerce brands, industrial parts distributors, specialty food and beverage importers — who move regular container volume but have no in-house trade compliance counsel and simply accept whatever HTS classification their customs broker used.

Pain Point

Under the 2025–2026 tariff environment — broad Section 301 and IEEPA duties layered on shifting country-specific rates and exclusion lists — customs brokers routinely default to conservative, higher-duty HTS codes to limit their own liability rather than research the more specific classification, applicable exclusion, or First Sale valuation a product actually qualifies for. That leaves real, recoverable duty sitting on the table. Recovering it requires filing a Post Summary Correction within 300 days of entry, or a formal Protest within 180 days of liquidation — a document-heavy, deadline-bound process most SMB importers don't even know exists, so the money simply expires unclaimed.

MVP Workflow

  1. Importer uploads their entry summary data (ABI broker export or an ACE portal CSV) — commercial invoices, HTS codes used, and duty paid per entry.
  2. For each SKU, the system pulls the product spec sheet or description and matches it against CBP's CROSS ruling database and the Harmonized Tariff Schedule to surface a more specific, lower-duty classification, an applicable exclusion, or a First Sale valuation opportunity.
  3. Each flagged entry gets a savings-confidence score with the exact CBP ruling(s) it's grounded in, so the claim is citable, not just asserted.
  4. A deadline tracker shows which entries are still inside the 300-day Post Summary Correction window versus the 180-day post-liquidation protest window versus already time-barred, with alerts before each closes.
  5. For flagged entries, the system auto-generates the PSC filing packet or protest letter with ruling citations, ready for the importer or their broker to submit through ACE.
  6. Recovered refunds are tracked back to the flagged entries, and outcomes feed the ruling-match model to sharpen future confidence scores.

Data & Integrations Needed

CBP CROSS ruling database (public) USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule ACE portal / ABI broker entry-summary exports Section 301 & IEEPA exclusion lists Product spec sheet OCR/parsing ERP/accounting hooks (NetSuite, QuickBooks) for SKU data

Pricing Model

Contingency fee of 20–30% of duty actually recovered — zero cost to the importer if nothing is found, which removes the adoption barrier entirely. An optional $199/mo subscription adds forward-looking classification review on new SKUs and entries, so future shipments stop overpaying in the first place.

First Version Scope

Cover Post Summary Correction only, for entries still inside the 300-day window (skip formal Protests and Court of International Trade appeals for v1). Support one entry-summary export format to start, matched to the top 2–3 customs brokers by SMB market share, with manual CSV upload only — no live ACE API integration yet. Go to market through customs broker referral partnerships and trade-compliance LinkedIn communities rather than paid ads.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Trade compliance software has been built almost entirely for large enterprises as GTM (global trade management) suites bolted onto SAP, ignoring the much larger long tail of SMB importers who don't even know a duty-refund process exists. CBP publishes its full ruling corpus for free in a structured, citable form — exactly the narrow, high-precision domain where retrieval-based matching beats a generic chatbot. And the 2025–2026 tariff spike has made per-entry duty amounts large enough that a pure contingency model is an easy yes for the importer and a strong margin for the provider, turning a compliance backwater into a live "found money" pitch instead of a "yet another compliance dashboard" sell.
