SaaS Idea Log — 2026-10-24

BackflowTag

AI turns a backflow tester's field readings into the exact, correctly formatted annual test report for each of the dozens of water districts they serve — and chases every compliance deadline — so one-person testing shops stop losing evenings to paperwork.

Target Customer

Solo and small-crew certified backflow assembly testers (plumbers, irrigation and fire-sprinkler contractors with a cross-connection control tester certification) who test 500–3,000 backflow preventers a year across multiple water utilities and municipalities.

Pain Point

Every water purveyor requires an annual test report for each backflow preventer on its system, and each one wants its own form, portal, or emailed PDF, with its own due dates and failure/repair re-test rules. A tester working across 15–40 jurisdictions re-types the same readings (check valve psid, relief valve opening point, serial number, location) into a different template every time, then fields calls from building owners asking "did my report get filed?" A missed or rejected submission means the owner gets a water-shutoff notice and blames the tester. Utility-side software exists, but it is bought by the utility; the tester, who does the repetitive work and holds the customer relationship, has only spreadsheets and a pile of PDFs.

MVP Workflow

  1. Tester enters readings in a phone-friendly form after each test (or snaps a photo of the test-kit gauge and handwritten tag; OCR fills the fields). Device serial, size, model, and location are remembered after the first visit.
  2. System checks pass/fail against assembly-type rules (RP, DCVA, PVB, SVB) and flags anomalies such as a relief valve opening below 2 psid or readings identical to last year's, which utilities increasingly reject as copied.
  3. Based on the device's service address, the system picks the right water district and generates that district's required report: filled PDF, CSV row, or portal-ready field set, with a one-tap email send where the district accepts email.
  4. Failed devices open a repair-and-retest job with the district's grace-period clock, and the building owner gets a plain-language text with a booking link.
  5. Owners get a confirmation once the district acknowledges receipt. A dashboard shows every device as due, tested, filed, or overdue, and the tester can send a bulk reminder to owners before their due dates.

Data & Integrations Needed

Per-district report templates (public forms, built up district by district) Phone camera + OCR for gauge/tag photos Address-to-water-district lookup (GIS service-area boundaries) Email/SMS for owner notices Calendar sync for annual due dates QuickBooks/Stripe for invoicing (v2) Portal submission via RPA for the top districts (v2)

Pricing Model

$49/mo flat for solo testers up to 1,000 devices a year, $99/mo for crews up to 3,000, plus a $1 per-filing fee beyond the cap. A tester who spends even four hours a week on re-keying gets that time back for billable tests, so ROI is obvious in the first month. Optional white-labeled owner portal at $20/mo for testers who want to look more established than their competitors.

First Version Scope

One state, ten to fifteen of its largest water districts, and the four common assembly types. Mobile web form with photo-assisted entry, template fill-and-email output, device history, and a due-date board. No portal automation, no invoicing, no utility-side accounts. Recruit through state backflow tester associations, the ASSE and ABPA training communities, and local plumbing supply counters. Add a district only when a paying tester requests it; each new template is a one-hour job and a reason for their colleagues to sign up.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The incumbent products sell to water utilities, so the people who generate the data and feel the repetitive work have been ignored. The moat is the growing library of district-specific templates and quirks (which district rejects readings in the wrong unit, which wants the tag number on page two), which is dull data work no horizontal AI tool will bother to build. The AI is narrow and useful: gauge-photo OCR, anomaly checks, and matching an address to the right district, not a chatbot. Compliance is legally mandated and recurs every year for every device, so retention is baked in. Each tester brings their own customer list of devices, which makes the product a quiet system of record for a trade that currently runs on clipboards.
