SaaS Idea Log — 2026-11-12

VialTally

Automatically reconciles a vet clinic's controlled-drug logbook against its practice-management records and flags every missing milliliter before a DEA inspector or a diverting employee finds it first.

Target Customer

Independent small-animal and mixed-practice veterinary clinics in the US (1–6 DVMs, roughly 60% of the ~30,000 US practices) that hold a DEA registration and stock Schedule II–V drugs such as ketamine, hydromorphone, butorphanol, buprenorphine, methadone, and euthanasia solution. The buyer is the practice owner or practice manager; the daily user is the head technician who keeps the log.

Pain Point

Every controlled-substance withdrawal must be recorded with patient, drug, volume, and who drew it, and the running balance per bottle must match what's physically in the safe. In practice, clinics keep a paper binder or spreadsheet that drifts from the practice-management system (PIMS) invoices: a tech draws 0.3 mL for a sedation but the invoice says 0.25, a euthanasia gets invoiced but never logged, waste goes unwitnessed. Reconciling by hand takes hours a month, so it's done rarely — and discrepancies surface only at the DEA biennial inventory, a state board inspection, or after an employee has been quietly diverting opioids for months. DEA civil penalties run into five figures per recordkeeping violation, a lost registration can effectively close a practice, and veterinary drug diversion is a well-documented and growing problem. Automated dispensing cabinets solve this but cost $15K–$40K up front plus service contracts — out of reach for most independents.

MVP Workflow

  1. Clinic connects its cloud PIMS (read-only) and photographs or uploads its existing logbook pages; vision AI transcribes handwritten entries into structured rows (date, drug, bottle, volume, patient, initials) with confidence scores for human review.
  2. Going forward, techs log draws on a tablet/phone form in 10 seconds — scan the bottle's QR label, pick the patient from today's PIMS appointments, enter volume, second user signs for waste.
  3. Nightly, a reconciliation engine matches every log entry to a PIMS invoice line (and vice versa) using patient, time window, drug, and dose-per-kg plausibility, then rolls up a per-bottle running balance.
  4. Exceptions land in a morning queue: unmatched draws, invoiced-but-unlogged drugs, doses far above the clinic's own norm for that weight/procedure, waste without a witness, and bottles whose balance is trending short.
  5. A per-staff pattern view surfaces diversion signals that no single entry reveals (e.g., one person's draws consistently 15% over invoiced dose, or waste concentrated on their shifts) — shown only to the owner role.
  6. One click produces a DEA-ready biennial inventory and a printable, signed audit packet with the full chain of custody for any bottle.

Data & Integrations Needed

Cloud PIMS APIs (ezyVet, Shepherd, Covetrus Pulse, Digitail) On-prem PIMS via nightly export (Cornerstone, AVImark CSV) Vision/handwriting model for logbook OCR Distributor invoices (MWI/Covetrus, Patterson, Zoetis) for receipts DEA 21 CFR 1304 recordkeeping rules + state board variations Printable QR bottle labels

Pricing Model

Flat per-location subscription: $149/month for one DEA registration (up to 8 staff users), $249/month with diversion analytics and multi-DVM roles, plus a $499 one-time "back-log cleanup" that digitizes and reconciles up to two years of historical binder pages before an upcoming inspection. Positioned against a $20K+ dispensing cabinet or a single DEA fine, it's an easy owner-level decision.

First Version Scope

  • One PIMS integration (ezyVet or Shepherd, both API-friendly) plus generic CSV import.
  • Mobile-web draw/waste logging with QR bottle labels and two-person waste sign-off.
  • Nightly log-vs-invoice matching with an exceptions queue and per-bottle balances.
  • Handwritten logbook import with human-in-the-loop correction.
  • Biennial inventory and audit-packet PDF export.
  • Out of scope for v1: hardware, e-prescribing, state PDMP reporting, multi-location rollups.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Everyone building in vet tech is chasing scribes, client communication, and scheduling; controlled-substance compliance looks like a boring hardware problem owned by cabinet vendors. But the evidence needed is already sitting in two places clinics have — the PIMS invoice and the logbook — and nobody cross-checks them. The AI's real job isn't chat: it's reading messy handwriting and judging dose plausibility per patient weight, which turns a monthly chore into a nightly automatic audit. The buyer's motivation is also unusually sharp and personal: the DVM's own DEA registration and license are on the line, and the product doubles as a quiet early warning for diversion — something owners badly want but can't ask staff about directly. Each inspection season, board-discipline headline, or opioid theft at a nearby clinic is a free marketing event, and the per-registration pricing extends cleanly to dental, med-spa, and urgent-care practices later.
