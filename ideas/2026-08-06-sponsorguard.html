SaaS Idea Log — 2026-08-06

SponsorGuard

Audio-and-vision AI watches every sponsored post an agency's creators publish and flags FTC disclosure violations before the FTC — or the brand's legal team — finds them.

Target Customer

Influencer marketing agencies and in-house brand partnerships teams managing 20–500 active creators across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube on behalf of consumer brands (beauty, supplements, fintech, CPG).

Pain Point

The FTC's revised Endorsement Guides carry penalties up to ~$51,744 per violation, and enforcement has shifted from warning letters to actual settlements. Compliance isn't just "did they use #ad" — it requires the disclosure to be unavoidable: verbally stated early in a video (not buried in a description), rendered in on-screen text large enough to read, and placed before the "swipe up" or "keep reading" fold on Instagram, not after. Agencies currently rely on a coordinator eyeballing a spreadsheet of post links once a month, or trusting the creator's own judgment. With hundreds of posts per brand per quarter across three platforms with different disclosure mechanics, violations slip through routinely — and because the agency (not just the creator) can be named in an FTC action, the liability sits with the exact team that has no scalable way to check it.

MVP Workflow

  1. Agency uploads a roster of creator posts weekly (CSV of links, or connects Instagram/TikTok/YouTube accounts via OAuth) tagged with which posts are paid partnerships and the brand's specific disclosure requirements (e.g., "#ad" in first 3 hashtags, verbal mention within first 8 seconds).
  2. Pipeline pulls each post's video/audio and transcribes it (speech-to-text), extracts on-screen text and its screen-time/size via frame sampling and OCR, and pulls caption/hashtag text via platform APIs.
  3. A rules engine — not a chat model — scores each post against the specific FTC placement/timing/legibility criteria and the brand's contract terms, producing a pass/fail per requirement with the exact timestamp or frame where it failed.
  4. Violations are compiled into a weekly digest per brand, with a screenshot/timestamp for each flagged post and a one-click "request creator fix" email template.
  5. Once a post is corrected or the disclosure window passes, the system logs the outcome so the agency has a defensible audit trail if a brand or regulator ever asks.

Data & Integrations Needed

Instagram Graph API / Meta Branded Content API TikTok Business API YouTube Data API Speech-to-text (Whisper-class model) Frame-sampling OCR / on-screen text detection FTC Endorsement Guide ruleset (static reference, versioned) CSV/contract-terms ingestion Email/Slack alerting

Pricing Model

Tiered SaaS billed to the agency: $299/mo for up to 50 monitored creator-brand pairs, $899/mo up to 250, custom above that — priced against the cost of a single compliance coordinator's time, and trivially justified against one avoided five-figure FTC settlement.

First Version Scope

Support Instagram feed posts and Reels only (highest FTC enforcement volume and simplest API access via Meta's Branded Content tools); defer TikTok and YouTube to v2. Manual CSV upload of post links plus brand disclosure rules, no OAuth account linking yet. Ship the rules-engine scoring and weekly digest only — defer automated creator outreach and the audit-trail export to v1.1. Distribute directly to influencer marketing agency ops/legal leads via LinkedIn outreach and marketing-compliance industry Slack communities.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Existing "influencer compliance" tools are mostly contract and payment management (CreatorIQ, Aspire, GRIN) with a disclosure checkbox bolted on — none actually watch the media itself to check placement, timing, and legibility, because that's a multimodal media-analysis problem, not a CRM feature. Framing it as a chatbot Q&A tool ("ask AI if this post is compliant") would undersell it and require someone to remember to ask; the defensible version is a silent pipeline that scores every post automatically against a deterministic ruleset and only surfaces exceptions — closer to a linter for regulatory text-and-media placement than to generic generative AI, which is exactly why agencies drowning in chatbot fatigue will trust it with something this liability-sensitive.
