SaaS Idea Log — 2026-11-05

SetbackScout

Upload a deck or addition sketch and an address; get a parcel-specific answer on which wetland, floodplain, shoreline and setback reviews it will trigger — plus a pre-filled permit packet — before the contractor quotes the job.

Target Customer

Solo and small deck, dock, pool, ADU and addition contractors (1–15 employees) working in regulated-land markets — coastal New England, the Chesapeake, Pacific Northwest, Great Lakes shorelines, Florida — and the small-town design-build firms and permit expediters who serve them.

Pain Point

Whether a backyard project needs a conservation commission filing, a floodplain development permit, a shoreline variance or just a plain building permit depends on overlapping federal, state and municipal layers: FEMA flood zones, National Wetlands Inventory polygons, 25–100 ft buffer rules, lot-coverage caps and zone-specific setbacks that differ by town. Contractors find out after quoting and signing — a surprise 8-week conservation review or a denied setback eats margin, stalls the crew and angers the homeowner. Permit expediters charge $300–$1,500 per job to do this lookup by hand, and most small contractors simply guess.

MVP Workflow

  1. Contractor enters the job address and uploads a photo of a hand sketch, a PDF plan, or just types dimensions ("14×20 deck, 6 ft from rear lot line").
  2. The system geocodes the parcel, pulls its boundary, and overlays FEMA flood zones, NWI wetlands, hydrography and shoreline layers.
  3. Vision/document extraction places the proposed footprint on the parcel and computes distances to lot lines, wetland edges and water bodies, plus new impervious coverage.
  4. A rules engine — built from the town's zoning and wetlands bylaw text, structured once per municipality — returns a checklist of triggered reviews with the specific bylaw citation and typical timeline for each.
  5. Output is a one-page "Go / Conditional / Stop" brief for the quote, plus a pre-filled permit-application packet and a site-plan overlay image to attach.
  6. When the project is borderline (e.g., 98 ft from a 100 ft buffer), it suggests the minimum footprint shift that avoids the review, with the resulting dimensions.

Data & Integrations Needed

FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (free) USFWS National Wetlands Inventory (free) County parcel GIS / Regrid parcel data Municipal zoning & wetlands bylaw text (structured per town) Sketch/plan vision extraction PDF form filling Optional: Jobber / Buildertrend export

Pricing Model

$29 per property check, or $99/mo for 5 checks per month and $249/mo for unlimited checks in up to 3 municipalities. A $149 "expedite" tier gets a human reviewer to confirm borderline calls. The unit price is easy to justify against one avoided blown quote or a $500+ expeditor fee.

First Version Scope

One state and roughly 25 towns, covering decks, sheds, pools and small additions only. Ship the parcel overlay, distance calculations and triggered-review checklist first; the pre-filled permit packet is v1.1. Municipal rules are hand-curated by the founder with LLM-assisted extraction from bylaw PDFs, then spot-verified against a few known past permits per town. Distribute through local home-builder association chapters, lumberyard counter flyers and regional contractor Facebook groups, and by publishing free per-town "what triggers a review" pages that capture search traffic from contractors.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The data is mostly free and public, but nobody joins it at the moment that matters: pre-quote, per parcel, per town bylaw. Zoning-lookup tools target developers and investors with big checks; permit-management software serves municipalities. The solo contractor in between loses money on every surprise review yet has no tool built for their question. The moat is the hand-structured municipal rulebook — unglamorous, town-by-town work that compounds with every job checked and every permit outcome fed back to calibrate the rules, and that big horizontal platforms will not bother to build.
