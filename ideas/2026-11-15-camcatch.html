CAMCatch

AI reads a commercial tenant's lease and the landlord's annual CAM reconciliation side by side, flags the charges the lease doesn't allow, and sends the audit request before the lease's objection window closes.

Target Customer

Multi-unit franchisees and small retail/restaurant chains (roughly 5–60 leased locations in strip centers and neighborhood shopping centers) — think a 14-unit quick-service franchisee, a regional urgent-care group, or a fitness franchise operator. They pay $20K–$150K per location per year in triple-net (NNN) pass-through charges, have no in-house lease administrator, and the CFO or controller handles leases between payroll and P&L close.

Pain Point

Every year each landlord sends a Common Area Maintenance (CAM), tax and insurance reconciliation demanding a true-up — often a five-figure surprise. Lease auditors routinely report that a large share of these statements contain errors in the landlord's favor: capital improvements passed through as maintenance, admin/management fees above the lease cap, wrong pro-rata share (gross leasable area changes, anchor tenants excluded from the denominator), missing "controllable CAM" caps, improper gross-up, or costs the lease expressly excludes. But the tenant's right to dispute is usually buried in the lease with a short window — commonly 60 to 180 days from receipt — after which the statement becomes binding. Big chains have lease-admin teams and contingency auditors; a 14-unit franchisee has 14 different leases, 14 different landlords, statements arriving on 14 different dates, and nobody who has time to compare clause 7.3(b) to line 42 of a landlord spreadsheet. Contingency audit firms typically won't take accounts this small, so the overcharges just get paid.

MVP Workflow

  1. Onboarding: tenant uploads each lease (plus amendments) as PDFs. Document AI extracts a structured "CAM rulebook" per location — pro-rata share definition, building GLA, admin-fee cap, controllable-CAM cap and base year, exclusion list, gross-up rules, audit-right window and notice address — and asks the user to confirm low-confidence fields.
  2. When a reconciliation statement arrives, the user forwards the email to a per-location inbox address (or uploads it). The system logs the receipt date and immediately starts the audit-window countdown, with reminders at 30/14/3 days remaining.
  3. The statement and its line items are parsed and mapped to the lease's cost categories; a deterministic rules engine (not a chatbot) runs checks: share-of-GLA math, cap arithmetic year-over-year, fee-on-fee (admin fee charged on taxes/insurance when the lease excludes them), excluded categories, and abnormal year-over-year jumps per line.
  4. Each flag shows the exact lease clause, the statement line, the estimated dollar overcharge, and a confidence level — so the controller can see "$6,840 likely recoverable at Location 9, admin fee charged at 15% vs. 10% cap (Lease §6.4)".
  5. One click generates a formal objection and request-for-backup-documentation letter, addressed per the lease's notice clause, preserving audit rights before the deadline; letters and landlord replies are tracked per location.
  6. A portfolio dashboard rolls up exposure across all locations and forecasts next year's NNN charges for budgeting — useful even in years with no errors.

Data & Integrations Needed

Lease & amendment PDF extraction (LLM + layout parsing) Reconciliation statement parser (PDF / Excel) Deterministic CAM rules engine Per-location inbound email addresses County property-tax records (assessed values, for tax pass-through checks) QuickBooks / Xero (to match NNN payments to statements) Deadline engine with email/SMS alerts Letter generation & certified-mail API (e.g. Lob)

Pricing Model

$39 per location per month (annual billing) for lease abstraction, deadline tracking, automatic statement checks and objection letters — a 14-location operator pays about $6.5K/year, versus typical overcharge findings that can exceed that at a single location. Optional success-fee tier for customers who prefer no subscription: 20% of recovered amounts (well below the 30–50% contingency auditors charge). A referral partnership with lease-audit CPAs handles disputes that escalate to a full books-and-records audit, earning a revenue share.

First Version Scope

US retail/strip-center NNN leases only (skip office, industrial and ground leases, which use different expense structures). Focus on the five highest-yield checks: pro-rata share math, admin/management fee cap, controllable-CAM cap, excluded-cost categories, and year-over-year anomalies. Manual upload plus email forwarding; no landlord portal integrations. Human-in-the-loop review of every lease abstraction for the first 50 customers to build a labeled clause dataset. Sell through franchisee associations (independent franchisee owner groups for major QSR and fitness brands), restaurant CPA firms and franchise-focused CFO consultants, with a free "upload one lease + last statement, see what we find" teaser as the acquisition hook.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Lease-administration software exists, but it's sold to enterprises with real-estate departments, and CAM auditing exists, but it's a contingency-fee service business that only pursues large accounts — the multi-unit franchisee sits in the gap between the two, and it's a huge, under-served segment that buys through tight-knit owner associations where one win spreads fast. The value isn't "chat with your lease"; it's turning each lease into executable rules once, then running them automatically every year against every statement, with the statutory-feeling deadline (the lease's own audit window) as the purchase trigger. And the product gets sharper over time in a way competitors can't easily copy: because the same national landlords and property managers send statements to many tenants, CAMCatch accumulates a cross-tenant map of which landlords habitually over-allocate which cost categories — intelligence no single tenant could ever build.
