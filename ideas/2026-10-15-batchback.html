BatchBack

When an ingredient supplier's lot gets recalled, BatchBack traces it to the exact finished-goods batches and customers you shipped — and drafts the FDA-ready trace report and customer notices in minutes, not days.

Target Customer

Small food and beverage makers ($1M–$25M revenue): craft sauce, snack, bakery, cold-brew, supplement and pet-treat brands, usually co-packed or made in a shared kitchen, selling through distributors, grocers, and DTC. Typically run by a founder plus one part-time "quality person" who is really the operations manager.

Pain Point

Ingredient recalls (sesame, tahini, spices, flour, cinnamon, seed butters) regularly hit small brands second-hand: the supplier's recall email arrives, and the brand must work out which of its own production runs used the affected lot, where those units went, and whether it must recall. Today that means digging through paper batch sheets, receiving invoices in email, and spreadsheets while retailers demand answers within hours. FDA's Food Traceability Rule (FSMA 204) pushes the same record-keeping onto more foods, so the burden is growing. Enterprise traceability suites cost five figures and assume an ERP the brand does not have. The result is over-recalling (destroying good product) or under-responding (liability and retailer delistings).

MVP Workflow

  1. Brand forwards supplier invoices, COAs and bills of lading to a dedicated inbox; document AI extracts ingredient, supplier, lot code, and received date into a lot ledger.
  2. On the production floor, a phone-friendly form (or photo of the batch sheet) links each finished-goods batch to the ingredient lots consumed.
  3. Shipment data (Shopify, distributor POs, QuickBooks/ShipStation exports) is tied to batch codes so each batch maps to customers and ship dates.
  4. BatchBack polls FDA, USDA-FSIS and supplier recall notices and fuzzy-matches them to the brand's suppliers, ingredients and lot codes, including messy lot-code formats.
  5. On a match, it instantly shows affected batches, units shipped, and consignees, plus units likely still in inventory.
  6. It generates a one-page trace report, draft retailer/distributor notices, and a hold-and-quarantine checklist, all editable before sending.
  7. On a "no match" outcome it logs a dated clearance record, so the brand can prove it checked.

Data & Integrations Needed

FDA recall & enforcement report feeds (public) USDA-FSIS recall API Email forwarding + document AI for invoices/COAs Lot-code fuzzy matcher Shopify / ShipStation / QuickBooks exports Mobile batch-sheet capture

Pricing Model

$149/mo for one facility and up to 50 active SKUs; $349/mo for multi-site or co-packer coordination. An annual "audit-ready binder" export (mock-recall drill results plus clearance log) for SQF/BRCGS or retailer audits is included. Co-packers can pay for a white-labeled portal for their client brands.

First Version Scope

US FDA-regulated foods only (skip meat/poultry and export). Email-in lot ledger, manual batch-to-lot linking, CSV shipment import, and FDA recall matching with email/SMS alerts. Output is the trace report and notice drafts; no direct retailer submission. Run a "mock recall in 10 minutes" demo as the sales hook and reach buyers through food-incubator communities, specialty-food trade shows, and co-packer referrals.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Most food-safety software sells compliance paperwork; BatchBack sells the one panicked afternoon when a supplier's recall lands. The value is a reverse lookup (from a recalled lot to your customers) that only works if the cheap, boring front end (invoice email parsing and a phone batch form) captures lot data painlessly, which is where small brands historically fail. AI is used where it is truly good: reading messy invoices and matching inconsistent lot codes, not chatting. Because the same ledger doubles as FSMA 204 and retailer-audit evidence, a rare-event tool becomes a daily-use system of record with low churn.
