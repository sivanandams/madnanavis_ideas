SaaS Idea Log — 2026-09-11

DemurrIQ

AI cross-checks marine-terminal gate and vessel timestamps against ocean-carrier detention & demurrage invoices and the FMC's billing-disclosure rule to auto-flag overcharges and draft disputes before the recovery window closes.

Target Customer

Small and mid-size drayage trucking companies and non-asset-based freight brokers/3PLs (roughly 5–200 trucks or a few hundred container moves a month) who haul ocean containers to and from US marine terminals and get billed detention & demurrage (D&D) fees directly by ocean carriers.

Pain Point

Ocean carriers issue D&D invoices in bulk, and a large share reference free-time windows that don't match the terminal's actual gate-in/gate-out events, bill for carrier- or terminal-caused delay (chassis shortage, late vessel berth), or omit fields the FMC's 2024 D&D Billing Requirements interim rule requires to make a charge legally invoiceable (container number, applicable tariff, computation of free time, the specific dates and reason). Carriers give a narrow window — often 30 days — to dispute, and disputing means manually pulling gate-transaction data from the terminal operating system, cross-referencing vessel discharge dates, and writing a formal rebuttal citing the exact rule violated. A one- or two-person dispatch office doesn't have that time, and carriers hold future bookings and equipment access over the relationship, so the default is to just pay invoices that are, in fact, legally disputable.

MVP Workflow

  1. Company uploads carrier D&D invoices (PDF or EDI 210) along with the container numbers they reference.
  2. System pulls terminal gate-move events and vessel discharge/berth data per container from port community systems (eModal, PortOptimizer/GCT, Navis-based TOS feeds) or, where no API exists, terminal websites.
  3. A deterministic rules engine computes the actual free-time window and compares it to the billed period, flagging any charge that miscalculates free time, bills for a terminal- or carrier-caused delay, or is missing a required FMC disclosure field.
  4. For each flagged invoice, the system drafts a dispute letter citing the specific 46 CFR Part 541 provision and the exact timestamp discrepancy, ready to submit through the carrier's dispute portal or by email.
  5. The system tracks each dispute's response-SLA clock and outcome, rolling up a recovery dashboard of dollars disputed vs. recovered by carrier and terminal, so an ops manager can see which carriers systematically overbill.

Data & Integrations Needed

Port community / terminal operating system APIs (eModal, PortOptimizer, GCT, Navis) Vessel schedule & berth/discharge data D&D invoice parsing (PDF & EDI 210) FMC rule text corpus (46 CFR Part 541) Carrier dispute-portal / email submission automation

Pricing Model

Contingency-based: 20–30% of D&D charges successfully waived or refunded, billed only against confirmed credits, with no upfront subscription. An optional flat monthly tier suits high-volume shippers who want continuous monitoring across every invoice rather than pay-per-recovery.

First Version Scope

Cover the top 3–4 ocean carriers and top 5 US container ports (LA/Long Beach, NY/NJ, Savannah, Houston) where terminal data feeds are most accessible. Manual invoice upload rather than full EDI integration in v1. Dispute letters are generated for a human to review and send rather than auto-submitted, since carrier dispute portals vary and outbound automation carries real relationship risk this early.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Freight tech looks saturated because everyone's built another TMS or load board; this instead is a narrow, receipts-driven audit wedge that only became viable because the FMC's 2024 rule turned a vague billing dispute into a checklist of specific, citable disclosure requirements — exactly the deterministic, timestamp-matching problem AI is good at, with no need for chatbot UX to make it feel powerful. It also dodges the usual logistics-software sales objection ("we don't need another platform") by pricing against money the customer is already losing: the pitch is "we only get paid when we save you money," not another line item, which converts skeptical, thin-margin drayage operators far faster than a subscription ask would.
