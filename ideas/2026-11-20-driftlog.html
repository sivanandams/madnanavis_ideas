DriftLog

Turns an ag-spray drone's raw flight logs into state-compliant pesticide application records and a timestamped, weather-backed drift-defense file — automatically, after every job.

Target Customer

Small commercial drone spray operators in the US — 1 to 10 drone crews running DJI Agras, XAG, or Hylio aircraft under an FAA Part 137 certificate — who apply fungicides, herbicides, and insecticides for row-crop farmers, orchards, vineyards, and rights-of-way. Many are ex-farmers or ex-crop-dusters who started in the last 2–3 years as the category exploded, and they run the business from a pickup truck and a spreadsheet.

Pain Point

Every custom application creates a paperwork obligation: state departments of agriculture require commercial applicator records (product, EPA reg. number, rate, acres, field location, wind speed/direction, temperature, applicator license number, time on/off), and restricted-use pesticides add federal recordkeeping on top. Operators fly 10–40 fields a day in peak season and reconstruct these records at night from memory, a phone weather app, and the drone's controller screen — often days later. The real fear is drift: when a neighbor's soybeans or a vineyard shows damage, the state opens an investigation and the operator must prove wind, temperature-inversion conditions, buffer distances, and label rate at the moment of application. A sloppy record loses the case by default, and a single drift claim can cost $20K–$200K plus a license suspension. Existing ag-records software is built for ground rigs and farm owners, not for drone operators whose best evidence is already sitting unused in the flight log.

MVP Workflow

  1. Operator connects their drone fleet account (or drops exported flight-log/KML files into a folder synced from the controller) and sets up a product library by scanning pesticide labels.
  2. After each job, DriftLog parses the flight log: field boundary flown, swath, altitude, speed, flow rate, tank loads, and start/stop timestamps — computing actual acres treated and actual gallons/acre.
  3. It pulls hyper-local historical weather for the exact polygon and minutes of the flight (wind speed and direction at height, gusts, temperature, humidity, Delta-T, inversion risk) from a weather API and nearby mesonet stations.
  4. A label-rule engine checks the job against the product label (max rate, wind limits, droplet/altitude requirements, buffer to water or sensitive crops) and against mapped sensitive sites (organic farms, apiaries, vineyards from state registries like FieldWatch/DriftWatch) — flagging anything out of bounds.
  5. The system generates the state-formatted application record and a sealed PDF "drift-defense packet" (flight path map with spray-on segments, weather timeline, buffer map, label compliance check) with a hash so it's provably unaltered.
  6. Records are filed by grower and season; the farmer customer gets an automatic copy for their own records, and state annual reports export in one click.

Data & Integrations Needed

DJI Agras / XAG / Hylio flight log exports Historical hyperlocal weather API + mesonet data EPA PPLS pesticide label database Label-rule extraction (document AI) FieldWatch / DriftWatch sensitive-site registry State applicator record templates Mapping (field polygons, buffers)

Pricing Model

Per-drone subscription: $79/month per aircraft (seasonal billing allowed — operators can pause in winter), with a $49 per-incident "investigation export" for operators on a lightweight free tier. A $299/month multi-crew plan adds grower portals, multiple licensed applicators, and state annual-report exports. Upsell later: invoicing tied to verified acres, and a partnership channel with ag-aviation insurers who may discount premiums for operators with verified records.

First Version Scope

Support DJI Agras log exports only (the dominant fleet), and the 3–4 states with the densest drone-spray activity and clearest record templates (e.g., Iowa, Illinois, Arkansas, California). Ship log parsing, weather backfill, the state application record, and the drift-defense PDF; the label-rule engine starts as a manually curated set of the ~100 most-sprayed products rather than full label AI. No mobile app — a web dashboard plus a watched upload folder. Go to market through drone-spray Facebook groups, Agras dealers who sell the aircraft, state aerial applicator associations, and Part 137 training schools.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Ag software founders target farmers; drone software founders target mapping and flight planning. Nobody targets the compliance gap of the drone spray operator, even though it's one of the fastest-growing segments in agriculture and the aircraft already record more precise evidence than any ground rig or manned plane ever has. The product doesn't need generative chat at all — the AI is in label parsing and in fusing flight telemetry with minute-level weather — and its value is asymmetric: operators pay monthly for paperwork relief, but they stay forever because the first time a neighbor files a drift complaint, the sealed packet is the difference between a dismissed investigation and a lost license.
