SubmittalSnap

Pre-flight checker that compares a mechanical subcontractor's equipment submittal package against the spec section and flags every non-compliant line before the architect rejects it and burns three weeks.

Target Customer

Small HVAC, plumbing and electrical subcontractors (5–60 employees) doing $2M–$40M a year of commercial work, where a project engineer or owner personally assembles submittal packages for GC and architect review.

Pain Point

Every commercial project requires dozens of submittals: manufacturer cut sheets for air handlers, VAV boxes, pumps, valves and panels, each of which must match a spec section down to voltage, efficiency, NC rating, finish and approved-manufacturer lists. Reviewers reject packages as "Revise & Resubmit" for mismatches that were checkable up front, and each round costs 2–3 weeks of review plus a re-buy window. Rejections push equipment lead times (often 20–50 weeks for switchgear and chillers) onto the critical path, and the sub eats the liability for schedule slip. Project engineers spend nights highlighting PDFs by hand against 300-page specs because nobody has time to do it carefully.

MVP Workflow

  1. User uploads the project spec section PDF (e.g., Division 23 73 13) and the draft submittal package (cut sheets, schedules, data pages).
  2. The system parses the spec into a structured requirements checklist: approved manufacturers, performance values, standards (AHRI, UL, ASHRAE 90.1), accessories, warranty and O&M requirements.
  3. It extracts values from each cut sheet and equipment schedule (tables, nameplate data, selection-software printouts) and maps them to the matching spec items.
  4. Output is a compliance matrix: pass, fail, missing or ambiguous per requirement, with the source page highlighted on both the spec and the cut sheet.
  5. It also cross-checks the package against the Division 01 submittal procedure (required transmittal fields, number of copies, deviation-list format) and the contract's equipment schedule on the drawings.
  6. One click exports a marked-up PDF with a "Deviations from Spec" cover page the sub can send, so honest substitution requests go in up front rather than being caught as errors.

Data & Integrations Needed

Spec & cut-sheet PDFs (user upload) Layout-aware PDF table extraction CSI MasterFormat section templates Manufacturer product-data lookups (public catalogs, AHRI directory) Procore / Autodesk Build submittal export (v2)

Pricing Model

$149/month per active project (unlimited checks on that project), or $19 per submittal package for occasional users. A $499/month company plan covers unlimited projects and adds a shared library of reusable, pre-parsed spec checklists. The price is a rounding error against a single week of schedule delay.

First Version Scope

Only HVAC equipment in four spec categories (air handlers, VAV terminals, pumps, fans) in one market, US commercial work. Upload-only, no project-management integrations. Human-verifiable output: every verdict links back to the exact page and line it came from, and anything below a confidence threshold is shown as "ambiguous" rather than guessed. Validate by running it against 20 past rejected submittals from two friendly subs and measuring how many of the reviewer's comments it would have caught. Go to market through local mechanical contractor associations (MCA/SMACNA chapters) and project-engineer communities.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Construction software crowds around the general contractor and the owner: Procore, Autodesk and the reviewer-side tools that route submittals. Almost nothing serves the sub who prepares them, and that is where the avoidable error originates. The product is also a structured-data comparison problem, spec constraints versus extracted product values, so a deterministic compliance matrix with source citations is the right interface and a chat window would be worse. The buyer is the person who personally absorbs the cost of each rejection, which makes the sale fast, and the output (a deviations cover page) is something the sub would otherwise skip writing, which also reduces their contractual exposure.
