CarnetCrew

Turns a touring band's or trade-show exhibitor's messy gear list into a customs-ready ATA Carnet "General List" with valuations, serial numbers and country-specific red flags, so gear isn't seized at the border.

Target Customer

Small and mid-size touring acts, their tour managers and production companies, plus boutique trade-show exhibitors and film/photo crews, who temporarily export equipment worth $20K–$500K abroad several times a year and currently hire a carnet broker or wing it with spreadsheets.

Pain Point

An ATA Carnet is a passport for goods, and one wrong line (a missing serial number, an item that isn't on the list, a mismatched value or country of origin, a consumable that doesn't qualify) can mean gear held at customs, tour dates lost, or a claim against the carnet's bond for duties up to 100%+ of value. Brokers charge hundreds of dollars per application, take days to turn it around, and rebuild the list from scratch each tour even though 90% of the gear is identical. Last-minute changes (a borrowed amp, new merch, a swapped laptop) happen on the road and are usually never reconciled, which causes the very problems at re-import that the carnet was meant to prevent.

MVP Workflow

  1. User uploads whatever they have: a tech rider, a Google Sheet of gear, an insurance schedule PDF, or phone photos of cases and labels.
  2. Extraction pulls out make, model, serial number, quantity, weight, country of origin and replacement value, and flags items with missing or suspicious fields (no serial, round-number valuation, duplicate serials).
  3. The tool assigns an HS code to each item and builds a carnet-format General List, then runs a destination-country check: carnet-ineligible items (consumables, merch for sale, giveaways), countries that need extra paperwork, and ATA Convention member status.
  4. A packing-case view pairs each item with a case and produces a scannable checklist so the crew can verify the physical gear against the list at departure and at return.
  5. On the road, a mobile page lets the tour manager log additions or removals; the tool shows which changes need an amended carnet versus a separate temporary-import entry.
  6. The export is a broker-ready, issuing-chamber-formatted package (PDF plus CSV) that the user submits to their chamber or broker, which typically cuts the broker's work from hours to minutes.

Data & Integrations Needed

Harmonized System code database ATA Carnet member-country rules (public) Document and photo OCR for serial numbers Google Sheets / CSV import Insurance equipment schedule parsing Mobile-friendly scan and checklist UI

Pricing Model

$49 per carnet package for one-off users. A $19/mo "gear vault" subscription keeps the reusable inventory, case manifests and change history, which is where repeat value sits, since the same list is reused each tour. A $149/mo broker plan adds client workspaces and white-labeled exports for customs brokers and chambers serving many small clients.

First Version Scope

Cover US-issued carnets for music and trade-show gear to a short list of high-traffic destinations (UK, EU member states, Canada, Japan, Australia). No filing or payment integration in v1: the output is a clean, validated General List and checklist the user hands to their issuing chamber or broker. Skip film/TV, commercial samples and professional equipment edge cases until the music and exhibitor flow is validated. Go to market through tour-manager communities, booking-agent newsletters, and backline and rental companies who recommend tools to their clients.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Everyone assumes the pain is the government form; the real pain is the inventory data behind it. The product wins by making the gear list a living, serial-level asset that doubles as an insurance schedule and a packing checklist, and the carnet is just one export of it. The buyer is also split: musicians pay once out of urgency, but brokers and chambers have a structural incentive to adopt it because clean inputs make their margins better, which gives a solo founder a built-in distribution channel. It rewards document extraction and rules validation rather than chatbot UX, and the niche is too small for the big logistics and compliance platforms to bother with.
