CamSleuth

AI reads a small retail or medical tenant's commercial lease against the landlord's annual CAM reconciliation, finds the overcharges, and sends the audit-rights notice before the lease's review window closes.

Target Customer

Small commercial tenants in NNN or modified-gross leases who receive an annual Common Area Maintenance (CAM) / operating-expense reconciliation: multi-unit franchisees (QSR, fitness, salons), dental and veterinary practices, urgent-care clinics, and independent retailers in strip centers and medical office buildings — typically paying $3–$15/sq ft in pass-through charges on 1,500–10,000 sq ft spaces.

Pain Point

Every spring, landlords send a reconciliation "true-up" bill — often $2K–$20K — that tenants pay because checking it means re-reading a 60-page lease and cross-referencing definitions of Operating Expenses, exclusions, caps on controllable expenses, management-fee percentages, gross-up clauses, and pro-rata share denominators. Errors are common and systematically favor the landlord: capital improvements passed through as repairs, management fees charged on top of excluded items, pro-rata shares calculated on leased rather than total GLA, cap violations, and expenses belonging to anchor tenants. Lease-audit firms do catch this, but they work on 30–50% contingency and only take tenants with large enough recoveries — national chains, not the 4-location franchisee. Worse, most leases give the tenant a short audit-or-dispute window (often 60–180 days after the statement), so by the time an owner thinks about it, the right has lapsed.

MVP Workflow

  1. Tenant uploads the lease (plus amendments) and the landlord's reconciliation statement and expense backup, or forwards the landlord's email to a dedicated inbox address.
  2. Document AI extracts the lease's expense rules into a structured "lease rulebook": OpEx definition, explicit exclusions, controllable-expense cap and base year, admin/management fee %, gross-up rule, pro-rata share formula and stated square footage, and the audit/dispute deadline.
  3. A countdown starts immediately for the dispute window, with email/SMS reminders at 30/14/3 days.
  4. The reconciliation line items are classified (repair vs. capital, controllable vs. uncontrollable, excluded categories) and a deterministic rules engine recomputes the tenant's share, producing a variance table: each flagged line, the lease clause it violates, and the dollar impact.
  5. Pro-rata share is sanity-checked against the center's total GLA pulled from county assessor records and the landlord's prior-year statements, catching denominator shrinkage.
  6. The tenant gets a plain-English findings report and a one-click, lease-cited letter that invokes audit rights or requests backup documentation, sent before the deadline; strong cases can be routed to a partner lease-audit CPA for a referral fee.

Data & Integrations Needed

Document AI for lease & reconciliation PDFs Lease-clause extraction schema Expense classification model (capital vs. repair) Deterministic recomputation engine County assessor / parcel GLA data Email ingest inbox QuickBooks / Xero (to pull past CAM payments) Deadline alerts (email/SMS)

Pricing Model

$249 flat per lease-year review (includes the dispute letter), or $49/location/month for continuous monitoring across all leases with automatic review of every new statement. An optional success tier of 15% of recovered dollars applies only when the tenant uses the escalation/negotiation assist — far below the 30–50% audit firms charge, and available to tenants those firms ignore. Franchisor and dental-DSO channel plans priced per location.

First Version Scope

U.S. retail strip-center and medical-office NNN leases only (skip office full-service-gross leases, ground leases, and percentage-rent audits). Support the ten highest-frequency error types: cap violations, management fee on excluded items, capital-expense pass-through, pro-rata denominator, gross-up misuse, insurance/tax double counting, anchor-tenant carve-outs, late-statement waivers, base-year errors, and arithmetic errors. Manual upload plus email forwarding; a human reviewer QA's every report for the first 100 customers to build a labeled dataset. Go to market through franchisee associations, dental practice-management consultants, and tenant-rep brokers who want a value-add for clients at renewal time.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The money is already gone — the tenant paid it — so nobody frames this as a software problem; it lives in the "hire an auditor someday" bucket that small tenants never reach. But the core of the job is mechanical: once a lease's expense clauses are turned into structured rules, the recomputation is deterministic math, and AI's role is narrow and verifiable (extraction and line-item classification), not open-ended chat. The lease's dispute deadline creates an annual, calendar-driven purchase trigger with a dollar number attached, and the product compounds: every lease reviewed sharpens the classification model, a tenant's prior-year findings make the next year's review sharper, and aggregated anonymized data reveals which landlords and property managers overcharge most — a dataset tenant-rep brokers would pay for at lease negotiation.
