SaaS Idea Log — 2026-08-07

ProtestClock

AI screens a losing federal contractor's debriefing letter against GAO bid-protest precedent, flags a protestable defect, and drafts the protest before the 10-day statutory clock runs out.

Target Customer

Small and mid-size federal government contractors — especially 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, and HUBZone set-aside firms — who just lost a federal solicitation and received (or can request) an agency debriefing, typically on contracts in the $500K–$50M range where a re-compete loss meaningfully hurts revenue.

Pain Point

After a federal bid loss, the contracting officer's debriefing starts a brutally tight clock: 3 days to request it, and then just 10 calendar days from the debriefing to file a GAO protest and get the automatic stay of award. GAO's own statistics show a large share of protests that reach a merits decision are sustained or trigger corrective action — meaning many "losses" were actually procurement errors (unequal treatment, unstated evaluation criteria, flawed cost-realism analysis, inconsistent past-performance scoring). But spotting those errors requires reading the debriefing against FAR Subpart 15 and years of GAO decisions, and a protest attorney costs $15K–$50K with a multi-day intake process — money and time most small contractors don't have inside a 10-day window. So the default move, even when a company was cheated out of a fair evaluation, is to walk away.

MVP Workflow

  1. Contractor uploads the debriefing letter/slides, the solicitation's Section L/M (evaluation criteria), and their own proposal's relevant excerpts, and enters the debriefing date.
  2. System immediately starts a countdown showing the request-for-debriefing deadline, the 10-day GAO protest deadline, and the agency-level protest alternative deadline, and sends email/SMS alerts as each approaches.
  3. Document AI extracts the stated evaluation factors, the ratings/scores given to the contractor, and any comparative information disclosed about the awardee (price, technical rating, past performance).
  4. A retrieval engine matches the extracted facts against a tagged corpus of published GAO sustained-protest decisions, surfacing specific precedent (e.g., "GAO sustained a protest on materially similar unequal-treatment facts in B-XXXXXX") for each flagged issue.
  5. The system outputs a scored issue report (which grounds are present, strength of the precedent match, and citations) plus a first-draft protest memorandum formatted for GAO's Electronic Protest Docketing System, ready for the contractor or a reviewing attorney to finalize.
  6. If no strong ground is found, the report says so plainly and recommends against protesting — a fast, cheap "don't waste your money" answer is itself the product for a large share of users.

Data & Integrations Needed

GAO bid protest decisions (public, via GAO's EPDS/EDS) FAR Subpart 15 & Part 33 text SAM.gov / USASpending contract award data Document AI for debriefing & solicitation parsing Precedent-tagging & retrieval index Deadline engine with email/SMS alerts

Pricing Model

Flat per-review fee of $299–$499 with a guaranteed 24-hour turnaround, purchased at the moment of urgency rather than as a subscription. An optional $79/mo "award watch" add-on monitors the contractor's SAM.gov bid activity and reminds them to request a debriefing the moment an award is posted, so they never miss the initial 3-day trigger.

First Version Scope

Cover GAO-level protests only (skip Court of Federal Claims and agency-level protests for v1), and only full-and-open or set-aside negotiated procurements under FAR Part 15 (skip task-order protests under multi-award IDIQs, which have different deadlines and a $10M jurisdictional threshold). Ship the deadline countdown and issue-flagging report first; the drafted protest memo can be a v1.1 addition once the flagging accuracy is validated against real GAO outcomes. Manual document upload only. Go to market through APEX Accelerator (PTAC) counselors, govcon LinkedIn communities, and small-business federal contracting associations like NCMA and WOSB/SDVOSB chapters.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

GAO publishes the full text of every bid protest decision for free, which is exactly the kind of structured, citable legal corpus that rewards precedent-matching over generic chatbot Q&A — yet no product treats it as a real-time triage engine for the specific small businesses who most need it. The category is also self-selecting for willingness to pay: by definition, every user just lost a contract worth six or seven figures, and the statutory 10-day fuse turns "maybe someday" into "decide by Thursday," which is a far stronger purchase trigger than most compliance tooling gets. And because the honest answer is "don't protest" more often than not, the product's credibility — and repeat use across a contractor's pipeline of bids — comes precisely from how often it talks a user out of a claim, not into one.
