HoodProof

Photo-verified NFPA 96 kitchen-exhaust cleaning records for hood-cleaning contractors — an insurer-ready compliance file for every restaurant they service, built from the technician's phone, with no paperwork.

Target Customer

Owner-operated kitchen exhaust (hood) cleaning companies with 2–25 technicians serving restaurants, hotels, hospitals and school cafeterias. Secondary buyers later: restaurant groups and facility managers who need proof for their insurers and fire marshals.

Pain Point

NFPA 96 requires commercial cooking exhaust systems to be cleaned on a fixed schedule (as often as monthly for high-volume or solid-fuel cooking), and the cleaner must leave a service sticker and a report. After a duct fire, the restaurant’s insurer and the fire marshal ask for proof of cleaning — and the cleaning company is often blamed when records are thin. Today records are a sticker, a carbon-copy invoice and a folder of unlabeled phone photos. Techs skip hard-to-reach sections (fans, plenums, vertical risers), owners can’t prove what was and wasn’t accessible, and inspectors reject vague reports. One disputed fire claim can cost a small contractor its liability coverage — or the business.

MVP Workflow

  1. Owner imports customer sites and each system’s required cleaning frequency (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual) from a CSV or QuickBooks invoices.
  2. Tech opens the job on a phone and is guided through a fixed shot list: hood interior, filters, plenum, duct run, access panels, fan/rooftop, with before and after shots. Photos are timestamped, geotagged and stored offline-first.
  3. Vision AI checks each photo for the right zone, blur and lighting, and compares before/after for visible grease residue. It flags missing zones, and prompts for an “inaccessible area” photo with a reason (no access panel, locked roof) that the customer can sign for on the phone.
  4. The system generates a branded PDF report plus a QR-coded sticker. Scanning the QR opens a read-only proof page for the fire marshal or insurer.
  5. A scheduler sends customers renewal reminders and flags overdue sites, so the contractor knows which accounts are drifting out of compliance (and which to upsell to a higher frequency).

Data & Integrations Needed

Phone camera + offline sync (PWA) Vision model for zone/grease classification NFPA 96 frequency table QuickBooks / Jobber / Housecall Pro import E-signature capture Public QR proof pages Email/SMS reminders

Pricing Model

$79/mo for up to 3 technicians plus $149/mo for up to 10, with a flat $1 per generated customer report beyond 200 a month. Optional $19/mo per customer for a “verified vendor” proof-page upgrade the contractor can resell to chain restaurants.

First Version Scope

Photo checklist, offline capture, before/after pairing, missing-zone flagging, customer signature, PDF report and QR proof page. Skip routing, invoicing and payments (integrate instead). Skip grease-level scoring in v1 and use only “photo present and correctly classified”, which is cheap and defensible. Target one metro area through local hood-cleaning Facebook groups, IKECA members and restaurant-supply distributors. Expect 20 paying shops to validate it.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Field-service software is crowded, but nobody builds around the evidentiary standard of a post-fire dispute. The buyer’s real fear is liability, not scheduling, and that makes a $79 tool feel like insurance. The mandatory sticker on every hood gives the product a built-in distribution channel: each QR code is seen by inspectors, insurers and the next competing cleaner. Photo records accumulate into a per-site compliance history that is hard to leave and becomes valuable data for insurers, who could later subsidize it. AI is used narrowly, to check photo completeness, not to chat.
