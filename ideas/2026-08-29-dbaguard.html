SaaS Idea Log — 2026-08-29

DBAGuard

AI cross-checks a subcontractor's weekly certified payroll against the exact federal wage determination for their project, catching misclassifications and underpayments before the Department of Labor's auditors do.

Target Customer

Small commercial trade subcontractors (roughly 10–150 employees — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, carpentry, general labor) working Davis-Bacon Act public works projects, who must file weekly certified payroll (Form WH-347) but have no in-house wage-and-hour compliance staff, usually leaving it to an office manager or outside bookkeeper.

Pain Point

Every worker on a federally funded construction job must be paid at least the rate and fringe benefit set for their specific trade classification in that project's wage determination — a locality- and craft-specific schedule that can run to dozens of classifications and changes contract to contract. Getting a classification wrong, miscalculating overtime on top of a fringe-inclusive rate, or filing a late/incomplete WH-347 isn't a paperwork slip — it's grounds for a DOL back-wage claim and, on repeat or willful violations, debarment from bidding on any public contract for three years, which can end a small contracting business outright. Yet the actual compliance work — matching job titles to the right classification, re-deriving the correct rate every week for the life of the project — is done by hand against a PDF, by people who are not wage-and-hour lawyers, for as long as the contract runs.

MVP Workflow

  1. Contractor uploads the project's wage determination (pulled by the tool from SAM.gov by contract/location) and connects or exports their payroll register (QuickBooks, Gusto, ADP, or CSV).
  2. System matches each project to its governing wage determination and lists the applicable trade classifications, base rates, and fringe requirements.
  3. AI reads each week's payroll lines, matches worker job titles and hours to the correct classification, and flags underpayment, misclassification, incorrect overtime base, or missing fringe-benefit credit — citing the specific line of the wage determination it's checking against.
  4. System auto-generates a pre-filled WH-347 (plus statement of compliance) for the responsible official to review and sign, with every flagged discrepancy highlighted for correction before submission.
  5. A running per-project compliance ledger accumulates automatically, so the contractor can hand over a clean audit trail the moment a contracting agency or DOL investigator asks for one.

Data & Integrations Needed

DOL Davis-Bacon wage determinations (SAM.gov API) Trade classification & fringe-rate lookup engine QuickBooks / Gusto / ADP payroll export WH-347 form generation CSV payroll upload fallback Per-project compliance audit log

Pricing Model

$199/mo per active public-works project (billed to whoever bears compliance risk on it), with volume pricing for general contractors or bookkeeping firms managing certified payroll across many subs. The recurring, per-project structure matches the fact that filing is a weekly legal requirement for the life of the contract, not a one-time check.

First Version Scope

Federal Davis-Bacon Act projects only for v1 — skip state "little Davis-Bacon" prevailing-wage laws, which vary by state and would fragment the wage-determination data model. CSV/spreadsheet payroll upload only, no live payroll integrations yet. Cover the five or six most common construction trades rather than all ~700 DOL classifications. Go to market through specialty-trade subcontractor associations and the construction-focused CPA and bookkeeping firms that already handle certified payroll for multiple small clients.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

Wage determinations are public, structured government data, but they're scattered across thousands of contract-specific PDFs that change with every project and locality — a messy lookup-and-match problem AI is well-suited to, and a poor fit for a generic chatbot that isn't grounded in the exact document governing that job. The compliance burden is also recurring by statute (weekly, for the life of every contract) rather than a one-off filing, which turns a compliance tool into a forced renewal rather than a nice-to-have. And because the failure mode — debarment from all future public contracts — is existential for a small contractor, the willingness to pay for a second set of eyes on a $199/mo line item is much higher than the dollar amount suggests.
