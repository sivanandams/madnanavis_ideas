SaaS Idea Log — 2026-07-19

WardLedger

AI turns a conservator's bank statements and receipts into the exact court-mandated accounting packet each probate court demands — so guardians stop risking contempt over spreadsheet errors.

Target Customer

Professional fiduciaries and family members serving as court-appointed conservators/guardians of the estate for incapacitated adults — roughly 1.3M active U.S. adult guardianship cases, split between individual family fiduciaries (usually one ward, filing rarely) and professional fiduciaries who manage a caseload of 10–100+ wards simultaneously.

Pain Point

Probate courts require periodic (usually annual or biennial) financial accountings itemizing every dollar in and out of a ward's estate — income, disbursements by category, asset schedules, bond reconciliation — filed in that specific county's local accounting format, which varies not just state to state but court to court within a state. Missing a deadline, misclassifying a disbursement, or failing to attach a receipt for a large expense can get a fiduciary held in contempt, personally surcharged, or removed from the case — real personal and financial liability. Family fiduciaries do this with a shoebox of receipts and a spreadsheet once a year and dread it; professional fiduciaries re-do the same manual categorization and formatting work, case after case, with no dedicated back-office tooling built for this workflow.

MVP Workflow

  1. Fiduciary connects the ward's bank/investment accounts (Plaid) or uploads bank statements and receipt photos for the accounting period.
  2. AI categorizes every transaction into that court's required schedule categories (income, medical, housing, distributions, asset changes) using a chart-of-accounts template matched to the specific state and county.
  3. System flags anomalies — large disbursements missing a receipt, categories trending outside prior periods, asset totals falling below the bond amount — for the fiduciary to resolve before filing.
  4. It auto-generates the formatted accounting packet: summary schedules, the county's official accounting form fields pre-filled, and an indexed exhibit list of attached receipts.
  5. Fiduciary reviews, edits any miscategorized line, and exports a filing-ready PDF; the system tracks the next filing deadline and sends reminders as it approaches.

Data & Integrations Needed

Plaid / bank & brokerage aggregation Receipt OCR County probate accounting form template library Transaction categorization engine Deadline tracking & reminders PDF generation

Pricing Model

Per-ward monthly subscription (~$39–79/ward/mo) for professional fiduciaries managing multiple cases; a flat per-accounting-period fee (~$299–499) for individual family fiduciaries who file only once a year or two.

First Version Scope

Manual upload only in v1 (no bank API): fiduciary uploads statements and receipt photos for one accounting period. Pre-built accounting-form templates for the five to ten highest-volume state/county courts by guardianship caseload (e.g. CA, FL, TX, NY, IL). Automated categorization, anomaly flags, and a filing-ready PDF plus deadline countdown. Recruit 15–20 pilot professional fiduciaries via the National Guardianship Association and state fiduciary associations, and a handful of family fiduciaries through elder law attorney referrals.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

"AI for legal" attention goes to law-firm-facing tools — contract review, e-discovery, litigation research — leaving this niche invisible because it sits at the intersection of family law, elder care, and bookkeeping, a combination no vertical SaaS company currently serves. Yet it has everything that makes a niche defensible: a hard legal deadline, personal financial liability for getting it wrong, and a task that's fundamentally about normalizing inconsistent formats and categorizing transactions — exactly what modern document AI is suited for, not a chatbot conversation. Elder law attorneys, who supervise and often recommend fiduciaries, are a natural low-cost referral channel that a generic bookkeeping tool would never think to court.
