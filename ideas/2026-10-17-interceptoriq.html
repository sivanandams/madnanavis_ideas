InterceptorIQ

Snap a photo of the pump-out manifest and InterceptorIQ files the right municipal FOG report for every restaurant a grease-trap hauler serves, chases the restaurants' overdue cleanings, and flags the ones about to get fined.

Target Customer

Owner-operators and small fleets (1–10 trucks) that pump grease traps and interceptors for restaurants, plus the 3–4 person facilities teams at small multi-unit restaurant groups that self-manage compliance across several cities.

Pain Point

Cities and sewer districts run fats-oil-grease (FOG) pretreatment programs that require every restaurant to pump on a set cadence (often every 30–90 days, or when the trap hits 25% capacity) and require the hauler or the restaurant to submit proof. Each jurisdiction has its own form, portal, or emailed spreadsheet, and manifests are handwritten carbon copies. A hauler serving 150 restaurants across 8 jurisdictions spends evenings re-keying tickets, and a missed or late report means the restaurant is fined or its permit is suspended, which means the hauler loses the account. Restaurants have no idea when they are out of compliance until a notice of violation arrives.

MVP Workflow

  1. Hauler photographs the manifest at the stop; OCR extracts restaurant, date, gallons removed, trap size, disposal facility, and driver signature, and the hauler confirms it in one tap.
  2. The system maps the restaurant's address to its jurisdiction and the rules for that jurisdiction (cadence, required fields, submission channel).
  3. It generates the jurisdiction-specific report (PDF, CSV row, or portal-ready fields) and queues it for email submission or one-click copy into the portal.
  4. A due-date engine computes each restaurant's next required pump-out from the jurisdiction's rules and past volumes, then texts the restaurant manager and proposes a slot on the hauler's route.
  5. A weekly exposure list ranks restaurants by days past due and by the fine schedule for their city, so the hauler knows which phone call to make first.
  6. Restaurant groups get a read-only dashboard and an exportable audit file for inspectors.

Data & Integrations Needed

Photo OCR for handwritten manifests Municipal FOG ordinance rules (hand-curated per jurisdiction, versioned) Address geocoding / jurisdiction lookup Email and SMS delivery QuickBooks invoice sync (optional) Disposal-facility receipt numbers

Pricing Model

$79/month per truck including up to 100 restaurant accounts, plus $0.50 per report beyond that. Restaurant groups pay $15/location/month for the compliance dashboard and reminders. Haulers can white-label the reminders under their own name, which makes the product a retention tool for them.

First Version Scope

Pick one metro and its 3–5 surrounding jurisdictions and encode their rules by hand. Support photo capture, OCR with human confirmation, report generation as PDF and CSV, and SMS reminders. No direct portal automation, no route optimization, no accounting integration. Sell by ride-along with five local haulers, then through the state restaurant association and the haulers' trade group. Expand one jurisdiction at a time, because the rules library is the asset.

What Makes It Non-Obvious

The buyer is a one-truck hauler, a segment that generic field-service software ignores and that still runs on carbon-copy books. The moat is not the AI: OCR is commodity. It is a hand-maintained, versioned library of municipal FOG rules and report formats that nobody will build for a general product because each city is worth a few thousand dollars a year. Fines make the pain concrete and the hauler's customers (restaurants) pay indirectly for the tool through retained service. The automation also produces a byproduct, a per-restaurant compliance history, which is exactly what inspectors and insurers ask for.
